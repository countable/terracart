

TerraCart

A gps driven farming rpg. Inspiration from Pokemon Go and Core keeper/Stardew valley

Core game loop.
1. Player should be rewarded for exploring a large area and finding objects which often correspond to real world map POIs.
2. Economic progression via farming, trading, hunting, exploring.
3. Safety - only encourage the player to go places that are actually safe to walk (avoid the need to walk on roads, water, private property)

Game objects:

# cells
The game is made of a grid of cells generated from vector tile data.
 - Some cells are tillable to replace with tilled soil.
 - grassland biome cells till in half the time.
 - roads should never have any interactables on them directly (only walkable paths, or sidewalk beside road)

# viewport
 - the player sees an 11x11 viewport, centred around them. Interaction REACH is
   a separate, smaller radius (2→5 cells) — see MOVEMENT & REACH.
 - generally, only update or consider timers of cells in the viewport, for efficiency. Don't update cells offscreen, but for timers just keep track of the timestamp of the last event. ie, for crops keep track of the time they were planted so later when onscreen you can check if they need to have sprouted when seen.

# interactables
Each cell may contain one interactable object (excluding fauna). ie, rocks, trees, crops.
 - bare hands work on any interactable; a matching tool relic only speeds it
   (timed actions use the WORK WHEEL; tool→activity map is the GEAR relic table).
 - state changes should have some visual or text indication.
 - other than house icons and fauna, interactables should fit in a single cell (should be clear which cell they are "in" and should only slightly poke out into other cells if at all)

# fauna
Entities that can typically move around over cells, like pets, enemies, wild animals
 - crows: move in pauses and straight lines. Orbit nearest crop at 1.5-3.5 cells,
   land on it for 2 full cycles, then destroy it. Scarecrow aversion 4 cells.
 - no fauna may move onto a building footing, or road. Nor is any SEATED on one:
   every spawner goes through WorldGen.isSpawnCell with the tile's roadMask.
 - animals simulate when within the SIM BUBBLE, 12 cells of the player's feet
   (CREATURE_SIM_CELLS); stationary beyond that. The viewport corner is ~7.8
   cells out, so the bubble carries about half a viewport of margin: a
   creature is already walking, hunting or inbound before it is visible,
   rather than starting the moment it crosses the glass. Measured from the
   player, never the camera anchor — a peek drag does not wake anything.
 - slime: a pest that drifts toward the player and leeches energy when adjacent
   (numbers + defeat in CREATURES). Tame with a sapphire (secret — hinted only
   in book tips); tame slimes don't drain.
 - deer (15 hp), crow (8 hp): sneak in and eat crops. Deer: 20% chance per wander
   step within 1.5 cells of a crop. Crow: orbital 2-cycle attack (see above).
 - DERELICT LAIRS ARE A HARD-MODE RULE (Difficulty derelictLairs, src/lairs.js).
   Every UNCLAIMED structure — a wrecked house, a sealed fort, an unclaimed
   castle, by the isClaimedKey test the derelict wash already reads — holds a
   garrison of IMMOBILE slimes. Two axes and no others:
     * building tier: house 1, fort 2, castle 3 (TIER_GUARDS, at the near ring)
     * distance from home: nothing inside LAIR_MIN_HOME_CELLS (12 cells), then
       a straight ramp to LAIR_FAR_M (1000m) where the figures are multiplied
       by FAR_MUL — a castle reaches LAIR_MAX_PER_STRUCTURE (15), a fort 10, a
       house 5. FAR_MUL is DERIVED (ceiling / biggest tier figure), not tuned.
   The rolled count is the cap less a seeded shortfall of up to LAIR_SLACK, so
   a house and a fort are always exactly their figure and a castle is 2-3.
   The KIND ladder rides the same ramp and is slimes all the way: surface slime
   near, cave slime from a third out, purple slime from two thirds.
   Distance is measured from the FROZEN starter anchor (save.starterCratesAt),
   never a Home adopted later — a moving Home would re-rank every ruin.
   Guards are seated on a ring one cell clear of the footprint, through
   WorldGen.isSpawnCell with the tile's own spawn options, so never on a road
   band and never on the building itself. A ruin with nowhere to stand holds
   fewer, never a guard somewhere wrong.
   GENERATED, NEVER STORED (the traps.js contract), and PER STRUCTURE: a
   garrison is seeded from its own building's identity — Lairs.structureKey,
   the footprint's centre in ABSOLUTE cell coords, the same shape a castle
   mints its claim key from — so it depends on nothing but the building. Not
   on the order the polygons arrive in (a rebuild that adds an Overpass
   building shifts every index), not on what a neighbouring ruin seated, not
   on which guards the player has already killed. Only save.caught reaches
   disk.
   NOTHING IS MATERIALISED AT TILE BUILD. Every tier-9 house is a wreck until
   rebuilt, so "derelict structure" is very nearly "building" and a city tile
   carries thousands — seating them all is tens of thousands of creatures on
   one tile. Instead the tile keeps only an INDEX of its eligible footprints
   (Lairs.buildIndex, one small record each, bucketed on a LAIR_BUCKET_CELLS
   grid, and SLICED at LAIR_INDEX_CHUNK footprints per pass because update()
   has no slicer of its own), and Lairs.stepResidency — throttled from
   update(), measured from the FEET — wakes the garrisons within
   LAIR_WAKE_CELLS (16) of the player and sleeps those past LAIR_SLEEP_CELLS
   (20). Live monsters therefore track what is AROUND the player, not what a
   tile contains, so a city block and a hamlet cost the same to stand in.
   The wake ring clears every other ring on purpose — the sim bubble
   (CREATURE_SIM_CELLS 12), the sprite cull and bow range — so nothing is ever
   woken in view or shot at before it exists; the wake/sleep gap is hysteresis.
   LAIR_LIVE_MAX (40) caps what may be woken around the player, nearest ruin
   first, and it only refuses NEW wakes: a garrison already standing is never
   un-woken to make room. A dead guard does not hold the cap shut.
   A ruin that woke EMPTY (cleared, or ringed by water) stays in the entry's
   resident set so it is not re-rolled every pass. A wound survives a
   sleep/wake cycle (an in-memory hp memo on the scene), so retreating out of
   the wake ring and back is not a free reset — a reload still heals, like
   every other creature.
   IMMOBILE means the movement step only: app.js wanderCreatures returns on
   `c.immobile` BELOW the leech and the monster attack, so a guard still drains
   you, still shoots if its kind does, and still dies and pays its bounty.
 - THE CROW PUMP IS A HARD-MODE RULE (Difficulty cropPests). On hard, a planted
   crow-edible crop with no wild crow already near the player dispatches one
   every ~90 s, seated at PEST_CROW_SPAWN_CELLS (10) — past the viewport corner
   but inside the sim bubble, so it is flying at the field from its first tick.
   On easy nothing is dispatched: the crows a tile spawns are the whole crow
   threat, and a field is only raided by a bird you planted next to.
 - cats (20 hp): tame cats chase wild crows within 8 cells, fight at 1.5 cells
   (both lose 1 hp per hit; crow flees, crow hp 0 → auto-defeat + feather drop).
 - dogs (40 hp): tame dogs chase wild deer and slimes within 8 cells, same combat.
 - wild deer and crow can be hunted with weapons.
 - each fauna can be tamed with a favourite food (slime taming: future work).
 - tame animals show a 💗 and wander within 5 cells of their release point; they
   can be petted (boost / follow in CREATURES PET).
 - damaged animals heal to full hp after 20 min of no damage (checked per wander step).
 - fauna move at ~1 cell per 5 seconds.

# biomes
Different map polygons rasterize to tiles according to rules 

## Residential
 - Small roads surrounded by housing.
 - Avoid interactables or fauna on private property. in residential areas, all interactables must spawn within 2 spaces of a road or path, or an area that is designated as public.

================================================================================
ONBOARDING (the first session)
================================================================================
 - OPENING: two story slides (trailer → wrecked neighbourhood), then the safety
   splash + "Go to my location". On a brand-new save the first GPS fix triggers
   a reload to anchor the projection, so nothing shown before it survives.
 - HOW-TO-PLAY CARD: shown once over the LIVE map on first arrival (after that
   reload), covering movement (you walk; WASD on desktop), reach, the farming
   loop, energy and home-only selling. Reopenable any time from ☰ "How to play".
   Seen-flag: localStorage terracart.howtoSeen.
 - OBJECTIVE CHIP: a HUD chip naming the ONE next thing to do, driven by
   quests.js STARTER_CHAIN — open a crate → till → plant → restore a wreck →
   harvest → sell at Home. Steps auto-advance on gameplay events fired from the
   sites that perform them (scene.questEvent); each pays a small coin reward.
   The chip retires when the ladder finishes or the player dismisses it (×);
   state lives in save.starter.
   The starter ladder is INDEPENDENT of the castle QUEST_CHAIN — the vault gate
   reads Quests.allDone(), which walks the castle chain alone. Do not merge them.
 - STARTER COMPASS: a light-green edge arrow (--green, the tutorial's
   colour — same as the chip's step tag and the step-complete toast) toward
   the ACTIVE ladder step's own
   space while the ladder runs (_starterGuidanceGoal): the nearest unopened
   crate for step 1 (the trail runs a full screen out but the viewport is only
   VIEW_CELLS across, so without a bearing the far end is invisible), the
   carved plot for "Break ground", the tilled-but-empty soil for "Sow a seed"
   (or the crates while the bag holds no seed — the seeds are in them), the
   nearest wreck for "Rebuild a neighbour" (or the crates while the bag can't
   pay the restore price), the planted crop for "Bring in the crop", and Home
   for "Cash out". Steps past "Break ground" used to all fall back to the
   nearest unopened chest_start_*, which — once the crates packed into the
   near TRAIL_SPAN of the walk and were all opened early — was the relic chest
   a screen away: the one arrow on screen led AWAY from the space the chip was
   asking the player to tap. Unresolvable targets still fall back to the
   crates.
 - THE CRATE TRAIL: the four supply crates are laid along the walk from spawn
   to the relic chest, evenly spaced across the NEAR part of it (TRAIL_SPAN =
   0.55) — roughly 2, 3, 5 and 6 cells out with the chest at 11. All four are
   met early, while a new player is still learning what a crate is, and the
   walk out to the chest is the clear stretch at the end. Each stop is in view
   from the one before, so the trail is followable: walk to the crate you can
   see and the next one appears.
   WHAT the walk is depends on the ground. A road or path passing very near
   the anchor (NEAR_ROAD_CELLS, 6) takes the whole trail onto its kerb: the
   crates seat down the road's shoulder walking outward — the chip says
   "supply crates were left along the road nearby", and the trail keeps its
   word — and the relic chest seats on the shoulder at the END of the line,
   about a screen out, so the crates still lead somewhere. Otherwise the walk
   is a 4-connected flood route from the anchor to the ring-seated chest, over
   ground a trail may cross — roads yes (people cross streets), water and
   buildings no — so it bends round a pond or a block instead of pointing
   through it.
   Contents run in the LADDER's order of need, nearest first — seeds (step 3
   sows one), then rockfruit and wood (step 4 rebuilds with them). The nearest
   crate held the wood until Aug 2026, which sent a player who followed the
   chip to "sow a seed" with an empty bag while the seeds sat at the far end.
   Crates seat on legal ground only (never the road band, never the trailer
   moat, never on top of something): on the kerb, always a shoulder cell; on a
   flood route, the route cell itself or one cell off it. Fallbacks: an
   undirected kerb walk along the nearest road when neither line seated
   anything, then a tight ring round the anchor.
 - THE RELIC CHEST: one real treasure chest (the trunk sprite, not a supply
   crate) seats a screen away from spawn — VIEW_CELLS cells out, so just past
   the edge of the opening view and clear of the cleared tutorial pocket — and
   only ever on a cell the anchor can be walked to, since a chest at the end of
   a trail is no use across a river. It holds ONE random wooden (T1) relic.
   The crates hand out materials; this is the only thing that hands out a TOOL,
   and bare-handed work costs 9s + 9 energy a swing where wooden costs 4s + 3. Slot and bearing are both derived from the frozen spawn anchor, so a
   tile rebuild reproduces the same chest with the same relic in the same cell.
   Its id carries the `chest_start_` stamp, so the crate compass points at it
   too. Never a downgrade: the payload is reconciled against what the player
   already owns (reconcileRelicOffer), same as any other gear roll.
 - NO PESTS AT HOME UNTIL THE FIRST HARVEST. Until the save's first crop is
   brought in (save.hasHarvested, stamped at the harvest site) the fauna
   spawner seats no slime and no crow within PEST_FREE_CELLS (20) of the
   starting anchor — the whole area the opening asks a player to walk. The opening is the one
   stretch they have no weapon, no relic and an empty bag, and a pest on the
   tutorial plot is the ladder being interrupted rather than a fight. It is a
   spawn rule, not a cull: the tile keeps its pest count (the cell is
   re-rolled), the rest of the map is unchanged, and it expires at the first
   harvest — the ladder's own proof the player has the loop. Baked in at
   tile-build time, so nothing pops into being the moment the crop comes in.
   Legacy saves that predate the flag: a PLAYED save is settled as already
   harvested (SaveMigrate.stampHarvested), so a veteran never wakes up to a
   pest-free home.
 - Saves that predate the ladder are retired on load (Quests.starterSkipAll)
   rather than being told to go till their first cell.

================================================================================
CORE
================================================================================
 - fullscreen mobile web first (desktop too), 390x844 viewport centered in the browser
 - vector map tiles from OpenFreeMap z14 (OpenMapTiles schema, no API key),
   decoded by hand (mvt.js) and cached in IndexedDB
 - Phaser 3.87 renders the world to a canvas
 - camera shows an 11x11 grid of cells, 6m each (~66m viewport). The player is
   fixed at screen centre; the world pans under them.
 - graceful network loss: a 'reconnecting' banner shows; cached tiles still
   serve from IndexedDB, and a service worker (sw.js) precaches the app shell.
 - EXTERNAL MAP DATA IS CACHED AGGRESSIVELY AND NEVER EXPIRES. A tile's bytes
   are kept in IndexedDB (and, at the HTTP layer, in the service worker's tile
   cache) and served from there forever, whatever their age. Age only decides
   whether to ALSO re-fetch in the background: past 30 days a refresh is fired
   while the cached copy is still what gets used, and a refresh that fails
   (offline, 5xx, rate limit) leaves the cached copy exactly as it was.
   Nothing in the game evicts a tile; only the menu's "Reset this game" clears
   the tile stores, on purpose.
 - a tile whose build FAILS is not remembered as failed: the half-built entry
   is dropped so the next pass retries (with a few seconds' floor between
   attempts). Before that, one flaky fetch left that tile blank grass for the
   rest of the session — the tile cache handed the poisoned entry back to
   every later request.
 - a tile that is already on screen is never taken away to be rebuilt. When a
   tile has to be regenerated (its real-world tree/furniture data arrives after
   it first rasterized), the replacement is built alongside the live one and
   swapped in only once it is ready; a failed rebuild leaves the original in
   place. Nothing ever reverts to grass mid-play.
 - in-memory tiles are LRU-capped (64); an evicted tile rebuilds from the
   cached bytes, and per-tile player state (opened chests, chopped trees,
   picked debris) lives in save.* so the rebuild reconstructs the same view.
 - all progress lives in one `save` object persisted to localStorage
   (debounced writes via save.js). Tiles cache separately in IndexedDB.

================================================================================
MOVEMENT & REACH
================================================================================
 - GPS (geolocation) drives the player on mobile. WASD / arrow keys move on
   desktop / localhost. The first manual key press latches a manual override
   for the session (GPS stops fighting the keyboard).
 - ONE movement model at every depth: a GPS fix (plus any stick offset, see
   STICK WALKING) sets a TARGET and the body walks toward it — underground
   mining through whatever blocks it, on the surface a plain walk.
 - a GPS GHOST (translucent, cool-tinted) marks your REAL position whenever
   the stick has walked the character more than a cell off it, so you can see
   where you are and walk back.
 - the MOVEMENT STICK (bottom-right) is always on screen and needs no relic:
   it walks you off the GPS target for stamina. Nothing ever replaces it —
   Dragon Powder and the Speed potion just lend it a better amulet tier (8 and
   9), coffee adds +1 tier on top of whatever's active for 3 minutes, and the
   ☰ "Free walking (debug)" toggle only makes it cost nothing.
 - device compass (deviceorientation) drives a facing-arrow indicator above the
   player's head; with no compass it falls back to the last move direction.
 - REACH: you can only interact with cells within your reach radius (cardinally)
   of the cell you stand in, measured from the player's FEET, not the sprite
   centre, so the reachable area is symmetric around the visible character. A
   white silhouette outlines the reachable cells; out-of-reach cells are dimmed.
   The silhouette, the cell-tap gate, AND the object/creature/treasure far-gate
   all read the same radius (coords.js reachRadiusM / cellInReach) so "if it's
   lit, you can tap it" — and growing your reach extends every kind of tap. The
   lit reach bubble IS the player's light: one radius does both jobs.
 - WHAT "DIMMED" COVERS: the out-of-reach wash, the underground torch wash, the
   low-energy pink tint and the white reach outline all paint onto a dedicated
   LIGHTING layer (app.js reachGfx), which sits ABOVE every ground layer —
   terrain fill, dashed grid, biome seam borders, the road band, pier planks,
   road lettering, POI
   halos, treasure pads, shadows, the biome haze — and BELOW the standing
   sprites. Ground recedes outside the lit area; trees, houses and creatures
   keep full contrast, and DISTANCE (not reach) is what dims them, via
   atmosFalloffGfx above the sprites.
   These passes used to paint into cellGfx, the bottom-most layer, so the dim
   could only darken the base terrain colour and every decoration above it
   stayed bright outside the lit area — biome BOUNDARIES most visibly, reading
   as glowing seams in the dark. Forcing the wash to alpha 1.0 showed the road
   strip, the ground decoration and the road lettering punching straight through a
   fully opaque black.
   Phaser draws in insertion order, so create()'s layer sequence IS the
   z-order and nothing in the code says so; tools/layer_audit.js pins it.
 - REACH SIZE: starts at 2 cells. Below 30% energy it drops one whole cell
   (floored at 1) AND the lit range tints pink to signal the fading light; at
   0 energy there is no reach. DEPTH also shifts the light: +0.5 cell on the
   surface, −0.5 cell for each level below the ground (floored at 1 cell), so
   the bubble tightens the deeper you descend — and the out-of-range mask darkens
   per level too. Reach grows to 5 via SIX +0.5-cell upgrades on one ladder
   (save.reachUpgrades, capped at 6 / 5 cells), claimed EXCLUSIVELY at the Wizard
   Tower's Inner Light — the FIRST rung of his ladder, bought with Discovery
   (see ECONOMY). The RING used to be forged by the same purchase; it is its own
   rung (Keen Eye) now.
 - WORK WHEEL: every timed action (chop / mine / fish / pick a worked crop /
   defeat a pest / catch an animal) shows a circular progress wheel at the spot.
   The matching tool relic's TIER sets the duration — it only SPEEDS the work,
   it NEVER gates it (no exceptions, no tool-or-tier gates):
     bare hands (tier 0) 9s · Wood 4s · Copper 2.6s · Iron 1.69s · Gold 1.1s ·
     Platinum 0.71s · Crimson 0.46s · Frost 0.3s   (bare hands = 2.25× wooden)
   The seven tiers are a GEOMETRIC ladder: each is ~1.54× faster than the one
   below, i.e. the two pinned ends (wood 4s, frost 0.3s) spread evenly over six
   steps. Retuned Aug 2026 — wood was 3s and the rungs between were hand-picked,
   which left copper→iron at 1.25× (an upgrade that read as no upgrade) right
   where a new player lives. Bare hands is NOT a rung on that curve: it's the
   always-possible floor, deliberately a wide 2.25× below wood.
   This single table governs chopping (Axe), mining (Pick), worked foraging
   (Pick/Axe), animal catching (Bug Net) and HUNTING game (crow / deer, sped by
   any weapon relic); per-activity sections below only note their unique drops /
   energy / tool, not the timing. EXCEPTION — fishing: the cast time is LOCKED
   at 9s bare / 3s with ANY rod; rod tier improves the catch table, skunk rate
   and energy per cast, never the cast rate (see FISHING). The tool in use is
   shown during the action. You wait it
   out; tapping anywhere cancels it (and refunds any pre-charged energy). The
   first 150ms is swallowed so the launching tap can't immediately cancel.
 - COMBAT WHEEL: fighting an ENEMY (see COMBAT) uses the same ring, read the
   other way round — the arc is the foe's REMAINING HIT POINTS and drains as
   you hurt it, green > amber > red. There is no duration: the fight ends when
   the hit points do. Damage rates are derived from the work-wheel ladder above
   (a weapon deals 15/durationSeconds HP per second), so a given weapon tier
   still kills a given foe in exactly the time the old timed wheel took.

================================================================================
ENERGY & FOOD
================================================================================
 - energy is a pool spent on hard actions and shown as ⚡cur/max (green >50%,
   yellow >25%, red below). Starting/base max is 100.
 - ENERGY_COST (items.js): till 2, plant 1, harvest 1, catch 5, rockPlace 1;
   picking wildplants/flora is free. Mining ore rocks costs 10 + (tier-1)*4.
 - ARMOR raises max energy: helmet +10/tier, chest +25/tier, legs +15/tier,
   boots +8/tier (per equipped tier). Equipping better armor bumps current
   energy by the delta too.
 - EAT: a persistent Eat button below the inventory eats the selected food for
   FOOD_ENERGY[id] (longgrass 2 … iceflower 150, cow 120, meat 45, etc.).
   Cats and dogs have NO FOOD_ENERGY entry — companions are never edible.
 - special food side effects: eating a rainberry waters every crop within 20m;
   eating a pairy points you to the nearest undiscovered chest for 5 minutes;
   eating coffee ADDS +1 amulet tier to stick-walking speed for 3 minutes —
   unlike Dragon Powder / the Speed potion (which override the tier to a
   fixed 8/9), coffee stacks on top of whatever tier is already in play,
   capped at the Speed potion's ceiling.
 - REST: energy regenerates over real time. Rates are "seconds to refill the
   whole bar" while you stand in a rest context (the pure formula lives in
   energy.js; app.js owns the per-frame accrual + splash):
     * HOME — on your starter trailer / adopted home shop: full bar in 90s.
       THE ONLY BUILDING THAT RESTS YOU. Standing on any building cell used to
       regenerate energy (300s to a full bar), which made a stranger's front
       room a rest spot and a town one continuous one; a home is worth going
       back to only if it is the place that does this. isRestingAtHome locks
       onto save.starterShopId — the trailer by its own cell, an adopted house
       by standing inside it.
     * CAMPFIRE — within 3 cells of a lit fire: full bar in 360s (slowest, but
       works out in the wild — see CAMPFIRE under CONSUMABLES).
     * OFFLINE — while the tab is closed, energy pro-rates linearly to a full
       bar over 1 hour away (energy.js Energy.OFFLINE_FULL_REST_MS), applied
       once on the next load.
 - EXHAUSTION: hitting 0 energy UNDERGROUND blacks the player out and wakes
   them on the surface (same world coordinates), costing HALF their money —
   floored, so it can never go negative and a broke player loses nothing
   further. They wake still drained, so changeDepth refuses to go back down
   until they rest (app.js _passOutToSurface). On the surface, 0 energy has no
   such penalty — it just blocks further energy-costing actions.

================================================================================
WORLD GENERATION
================================================================================
 - each MVT tile is rasterized ONCE into a grid of 6m cells (one terrain class
   per cell), then decorated with objects, debris, creatures, and treasure.
   Rasterization is deterministic per tile (seeded) so the same place always
   generates the same world.
 - terrain cell classes group into three base biomes; each subtype gets its own
   colour so the world reads varied but cohesive:
     ROCKY/URBAN: residential, commercial (retail/hospital), industrial, road,
                  path, building tiers, pier
     FOREST:      forest, orchard, wetland
     GRASSLAND:   grass, park, school grounds, sports pitch, playground, golf,
                  farmland (allotments lumped in)
   plus water and sand. Roads split into ROAD / ROAD_MD (avenue) / ROAD_LG
   (highway); buildings into BUILDING (house) / BUILDING_MED (fort) /
   BUILDING_LARGE (castle/civic).
 - building tier from polygon area + render_height: small house (<350m² & <10m),
   fort (350+m² or 10+m), castle (1500+m² or 15+m). Each tier still places one
   house/tower sprite, anchored on the footprint cell nearest the footprint's
   centroid (never off the footprint).
 - BUILDING FOOTPRINTS NEVER OVERLAP. Each building polygon is assigned an
   exclusive set of cells before anything is painted:
     1) a cell more than 45% covered by the polygon belongs to it;
     2) inside the bounding box of what (1) gave it, a cell's cover counts 1.3x
        over — a slight preference for squaring footprints off, so a rotated
        house reads as a building rather than a staircase of cells;
     3) a building that claimed nothing yet takes its single best-covered free
        cell (down to 15% cover), so a shed smaller than half a cell still
        exists instead of vanishing;
     4) a shape cleanup (drop crumbs, fill 1-wide notches, bridge diagonal-only
        contacts) that may only take cells no other building claimed.
   Every contested cell is decided by how much of it each polygon covers, never
   by paint order, so the result is identical whichever order the tile's
   polygons arrive in — that is what keeps a building clipped by a tile seam
   shaped the same in both tiles that draw it.
 - every tile enforces a minimum mix so trade/landmarks always exist: roughly
   ≥2% castles, ≥8% forts, ≥20% small houses — each rounded UP to at least one
   per tile (any tile with ≥3 buildings has ≥1 of each), re-tiered by area rank.
   Enforced over the buildings that actually landed a cell on the tile.
 - flat colour is drawn first, then a terrain sprite from the tileset is
   overlaid where mapped (grass, forest, sand, farmland, park), with auto-tile
   lookup so edges/corners pick the right frame.
 - building polygons → house/tower sprite; forest landcover → scattered trees;
   orchard → fruit trees; useful POIs → chests; fountains → wells; plus rocks,
   debris, treasure, and creatures (all below).
 - a satextract sidecar (data/) injects extra real POIs (individual trees,
   fountains, parking, street furniture) on top of the MVT layer.
 - NO SCATTER OBJECT SITS ON (OR BESIDE) A BUILDING. Building sprites are
   foot-anchored and their base overhangs the neighbouring cells, so worldgen
   keeps a one-cell moat clear of every scatter object around every building
   cell. The Home trailer gets the same moat at runtime (app.js
   clearHomeTrailerOverlap): it is a SYNTHETIC object that paints no building
   terrain, so worldgen's pass never sees it, and its art — 108×75 at scale
   0.6, centred on its cell — covers its own cell whole and reaches into all
   eight neighbours. Anything else in those nine cells is removed as tiles
   load, wherever the trailer stands (including after "Move Home here"). Real
   buildings are spared (deleting one would take its shop with it), as is
   ground flora. The starter crate trail seats outside the moat rather than
   losing a chest to it.

================================================================================
INTERACTION MODEL
================================================================================
 - EVERYTHING is tap-driven. Walking never changes a cell. A tap does EXACTLY
   ONE thing: handlers are tried in priority order (interact.js TAP_HANDLERS)
   and the first that matches consumes the tap and stops. Tapping the player is
   treated as a tap on the player's own cell.
 - A TAP IS A POINTER THAT DIDN'T TRAVEL. Dragging the map instead PEEKS: the
   camera slides off the player by up to 3 cells so you can see what the frame
   was cutting off, and springs back when you let go. A pointer that moved more
   than a few pixels taps NOTHING, however it ends — sliding the map off a tree
   must not chop it — so the tap fires on the pointer's release, not its press.
   The peek is a camera offset and nothing else: the body never moves, so reach,
   every tap gate, fog reveal and tile loading still measure from the player.
   Peeking at a crate three cells away and tapping it still says "too far".
 - TAP TARGETS ARE CELL-BOUNDED. Everything except FAUNA is hit-tested against
   the ONE CELL it stands in: a tap inside that cell hits it, a tap outside
   never does — however far the artwork rises above the cell (tall trees, the
   castle turret, a market stall, a house roof). Creatures are the exception:
   they move continuously and are drawn feet-anchored, so they're tapped by
   their DRAWN sprite box (see CREATURES). Multi-cell buildings stay fully
   tappable through the building-zone rule below, which is itself cell-based.
 - priority order (all gated to within reach of the player's cell):
     0)  cancel an in-progress work wheel
     1)  claim a treasure X on the tapped cell
     2)  creature whose drawn sprite contains the tap (defeat / catch / tame /
         feed / pet — see CREATURES)
     3)  pick the wild plant / flower rooted in the tapped cell
     4)  pick up a scattered coin lying in the tapped cell
     5)  world object standing in the tapped cell: chest, well, tree, fruit
         tree, mineral rock, ground stack, house/tower (shop), shrine
     6)  resolve the tapped cell (bail if unloaded / out of reach)
     7)  building-zone tap → a tap on any BUILDING cell snaps to the nearest
         house/tower sprite within 30m and routes to its shop (this is how a
         footprint larger than the sprite's own cell stays tappable)
     8)  release a selected animal onto the cell
     9)  pick up a placed rock / placed scarecrow
    10)  place a held rock / scarecrow on an empty tilled-able cell
    11)  break a natural rock cell (needs energy; pickaxe speeds it up)
    12)  planted cell → harvest / advance / water / nag
    13)  fish a WATER cell (bare-handed OK; rod speeds it)
    15)  tilled empty cell + seed → plant; with no seed selected it says so and
         LEAVES THE SOIL ALONE (the un-till toggle is gone — see FARMING LOOP)
    16)  untilled tillable cell → till it
    17)  non-tillable terrain → flavour label (water / building / road / path)
 - the auto-till fallthrough refuses to till any cell occupied by a wildplant,
   crop, placed rock, building, tree, or chest, so a near-miss never silently
   tills under something.
 - flash text fades up over ~0.9s for routine actions; bigger "loot pops"
   (pop-in, hold, slow fade) are used for chests, treasure-X, harvests,
   pickups, and catches. Loot pops are tier-coloured.

================================================================================
FARMING LOOP
================================================================================
 - all farming is tap-driven; tools are implicit (no need to equip a hoe to
   till, though a Hoe relic discounts it).
     1) tap untilled tillable cell → TILL (brown tint). Costs 2 energy (Hoe
        relic: each tier shaves cost and adds a per-tier chance of free tilling).
     2) tap tilled empty cell with a seed selected → PLANT (consumes 1 seed).
        With nothing plantable selected the tap only says "Select a seed from
        your bag first." — soil is NEVER un-tilled by tapping it. That toggle
        existed until Aug 2026 and fired on exactly the tap a beginner makes
        (break ground, tap it to ask what now), handing the plot they had just
        paid energy for back to grass under a message that read like progress.
        It was first suppressed during the ladder's planting step, which was
        the tell that the behaviour was wrong rather than its timing: soil is
        never in the way, so there is nothing an undo is for.
     3) tap planted dry cell → WATER (darker tint).
     4) tap the watered cell after the stage hold → ADVANCE one stage (dry
        again). Each stage needs one watering then a 15-min hold
        (Crops.STAGE_HOLD_MS); a watered stage auto-advances after the hold even
        while away.
     5) tap mature crop → HARVEST → +1-3 produce, plant destroyed, ~25% bonus
        seed.
 - crop grows over 5 visual stages (cols 0..4); 4 waterings to mature.
 - any tillable cell (non-water, non-building, unoccupied) can be tilled.
 - PRODUCE QUALITY IS THE BED'S, AND THE HOE SETS IT. Tilling banks the hoe's
   tier on the cell (save.tilledQuality, same cell keys as save.tilled);
   planting SPENDS it onto the crop as `qualBoost`. At harvest, quality raises
   the bonus-seed chance (+10%/tier over base 25%) and adds +floor(qual/3) to
   the produce yield. A tame butterfly pollinating a crop raises its quality to
   at least 1. (This was the WATERING CAN's until Sep 2026 — stamped at first
   watering from can.tier, +2 more while a 50-charge refill bank held. The bank
   retired with the bonus; see Crops.bedQuality.)
 - WATERING CAN (relic): HOW SOON, not what. Each watering has a tier/7 chance
   (Crops.waterJumpChance) of springing the crop a whole growth stage on the
   spot — nothing bare-handed, certain at Frost.
 - per-cell state: save.tilled (cell keys), save.tilledQuality (cell key → hoe
   tier), save.planted (x,y,crop,stage,watered_t,qualBoost). Tilled cells
   render a brown tint, darker once watered.

================================================================================
FORAGING / WILD PICKUPS
================================================================================
 - DEBRIS: every residential / park / forest / grassland / beach polygon gets a
   stable seeded density (~5-30%) and is sprinkled with one crop type:
     residential → mushroom (yards)      park → shrub       forest → shrub
     forest      → occasional nut + mushroom clusters (rarer, own RNG)
     beach/sand  → shell                 grassland → longgrass (Fern)
 - tap the cell a wild plant / debris stands in (and within reach) to pick it (+1).
 - some wild crops need WORK (a work wheel): rockfruit (stone) via the Pick,
   shrub (woody) via the Axe. A shrub yields WOOD, not a "shrub" item. Other
   wildplants are instant.
 - SURPRISE TREASURE: picking certain crops has a small chance of a bonus drop
   (e.g. rockfruit → gemfruit). Flowers pulled from flora polygons give 1
   'flowers'.
 - picked state persists in save.picked (stable per-cell id). Wild plant and
   fauna respawning is not yet implemented (future work).

================================================================================
MINING
================================================================================
 - all mining is via MINERAL ROCKS / ORE BOULDERS — objects spawned in clusters
   on quarry / industrial / residential / rocky (scree/cliff) polygons.
   Each is either a yieldTier (ore) rock or a plain "cave" rock. Any pick —
   even bare hands — cracks any rock (the Pick only speeds the wheel). Ore rocks
   cost more energy by yield tier, but there is no minimum-tier gate.
     - cave rock: quick + cheap, drops 1-3 rockfruit + a chance of coal, with
       a small per-tier chance of a lucky bar.
     - ore rock: drops coal + exactly one bar of its tier (copper/iron/gold)
       and, on T4+, a tier-rolled gem (sapphire/ruby/emerald). Work + energy
       scale with the tier.
 - density by zone: residential = sparse low-tier clusters; industrial = dense
   mid-tier; rock landcover (scree/cliff) = dense, reaches top tiers but
   weighted toward low.
 - broken rocks persist (save.brokenRocks) so they don't regrow.
 - YOU CAN ALSO PLACE rockfruit as a stone "fence" on an empty tilled-able cell
   (costs 1 energy) and pick it back up later.

================================================================================
CHOPPING
================================================================================
 - TREES (forest landcover + injected individual trees) chop via the Axe work
   wheel and drop 2-3 wood. Chopped state persists (save.chopped); stumps
   don't block tilling.
 - ACORNS. A fell also has a chance of leaving an ACORN — a sapling that plants
   a new TIMBER tree, so a wood you clear is a wood you can replant. The chance
   is the AXE's, and it is the axe's only reward besides speed and cheap swings:
   10% bare-handed (tier 0) up to a guaranteed 100% with a Frost axe, GEOMETRIC
   between (items.js acornDropChance, ACORN_P_BASE / ACORN_P_FROST).
 - PLANTING AN ACORN (items.js 'acorn', kind:'sapling' + plants:'tree'): tap a
   tilled cell with it selected, like any sapling, but it grows a `tree` (the
   thing you chop) rather than a `fruittree` (the thing you pick). It is stored
   in save.fruittrees with kind:'tree' and re-injected per tile by spawnInTile;
   felling it retires the record. It carries NO species, so it draws off the
   default growth sheet and takes no hardwood/softwood tier shift.
 - A PLANTED TREE GROWS ON THE CLOCK. util.js treeGrowthStage reads planted_t
   when there is one (PLANTED_TREE_GROW_MS = 4 days, the same window a fruit
   sapling takes): sprout → young at the halfway mark → mature at the window.
   One function, so the frame render.js draws, the size class the axe gate
   reads and the wood the fell pays all climb together — a sprout can't draw
   tiny and fell like a full canopy. A WILD tree still reads its static
   `variant`; nothing about it changes.
 - SHRUBS are wildplants (see FORAGING) — an Axe speeds the chop; they drop wood.
 - TREE SIZE & SPECIES (util.js) — a tree's render size, its wood yield, and the
   axe tier needed to fell it all derive deterministically from the tree's own
   fields (no extra save state), so the visual size and the gameplay size never
   diverge:
     * SIZE CLASS: bush / small / medium / full (large). Detected DeepForest
       trees carry a discrete crown class; size-less OSM/procedural trees fall
       back to their canopy scale — thresholded on the CROWN MULTIPLIER, with
       the per-species sheet base divided back out, so "maple draws at 0.85 and
       birch at 0.62" can't read as a bigger tree. A size-less MAPLE-sheet tree
       also draws a growth stage (sprout/young/mature off `variant`), so its
       class is capped by the stage on screen: a sprout gates like a small tree,
       not like a mature canopy. Required axe tier climbs one step per size:
       bush = bare hands (0), small = Wood (1), medium = Copper (2), full =
       Iron (3). Wood yield scales by SIZE: bush/small ×1, medium ×2, full ×4
       (the 2-3 base × the multiplier).
     * SPECIES SHIFT: pine reads as SOFTWOOD (−1 required axe tier, easier),
       maple as HARDWOOD (+1, tougher); every other species fells at its plain
       size tier. The shift moves the axe gate only — wood yield tracks size.
       (Maples also render ~10-20% smaller than their size class, a visual-only
       tweak that never changes the gameplay class.)
     * SHINY trees (see SHINY VARIANTS) always need a Gold (4) axe regardless of
       size and pay the shiny bonus on top of their wood.
     * HOME GROVE: every tree within ~100m of spawn is forced to SOFTWOOD
       (home.js HomeArea.softwoodSpeciesNear) so the opening wood economy — the
       starter blacksmith's first tools — is reliably harvestable bare-handed.
       Bush-tier crowns are exempt: they render as a uniform bush and are already
       bare-hands tier-0, so the pine stamp would only mislabel them — they keep
       their own species instead.

================================================================================
ORCHARD FRUIT TREES
================================================================================
 - orchard landcover spawns fruit trees that passively produce fruit on a timer
   (~30 min per tree). When ripe, tapping the tree picks the fruit (+1); the
   tree then resets its timer. No chopping required — fruit trees are never
   destroyed. Each tree's fruit type is seeded from its cell position (apple,
   cherry, peach, banana, orange, mango, coconut, apricot).
 - a tree that is BEARING wears its fruit: the fruit's own icon, drawn on the
   tree's canopy as a separate little sprite. Picking removes the fruit and
   leaves the tree exactly as it was — the tree's art never changes, so a
   worked orchard still reads as an orchard and what's ripe reads at a glance.

================================================================================
FISHING
================================================================================
 - THE STARTER POND: nothing about a spawn promises water within walking
   distance, so every save gets a 2x2 pond carved into the terrain grid TWO
   SCREENS from Home (app.js _carveStarterPond, POND_MIN_CELLS = 2 ×
   VIEW_CELLS, widening to POND_MAX_CELLS when the two-screen ring is all
   street or floor). It seats beside a POI chest when one stands in the band
   (within POND_POI_CELLS, never on the chest's doorstep), on walkable soft
   ground with a dry shore all round, never on a road band, a POI plaza,
   existing water or anything standing — and only where the anchor can WALK
   to (a flood that crosses roads but not water or buildings). It is plain
   WATER terrain: it fishes, refills the can and mirrors as cave rock like any
   lake. Frozen once in save.starterPondAt (top-left cell centre, the
   starterPlotAt convention) and repainted in place on every rebuild of its
   tile, sweeping whatever the rebuild regenerated on those cells. The band
   can cross a tile seam, so the search runs in world space over the loaded
   3×3 and defers (bounded) until the band's tiles have arrived — a plan drawn
   against half a map would seat it wherever loaded first, POI or no POI.
   Pinned by starter_pond.test.js.
 - tap a WATER cell → the Rod cast wheel, then a weighted catch. Higher rod
   tiers improve the catch and rare odds. Cast TIME is locked: 9s bare-handed,
   3s with any rod, regardless of tier (an exception to the WORK WHEEL ladder
   — tier-scaled 0.3s casts made high-tier fishing an unbounded money
   faucet).
 - per cast: a "skunk" (nothing) chance that falls with rod tier (T1 ~50% →
   T7 20%); else a weighted catch — mostly fish (minnow → bass → trout → salmon
   → goldenfish, rarer more likely at high tier), with a small relic chance
   (~2%) that follows the same milestone gating as chests. Higher relic tiers
   are possible but exponentially rarer; rod tier raises the odds of better
   fish, not relics (gear reward handled per GEAR). 6% per cast: an old boot
   (junk gag).
 - fishing costs 5 energy.

================================================================================
CREATURES
================================================================================
 - spawned per tile, seeded, biased to fitting biomes; only simulated near the
   player. Kinds: chicken, cow, cat, dog (domestic); rabbit, deer, crow,
   butterfly (wilderness); slime (pest). Chickens/cows wander ~1 cell / 5s near
   their home.
 - CATCHING vs TAMING are now SEPARATE (chicken/cow/cat/dog/rabbit/butterfly):
     * CATCH = a work queue. Tap with an EMPTY hand (or any non-food item). The
       animal FLEES the player at 2 m/s while the wheel runs; if it escapes the
       viewport the catch FAILS. Finishing it puts the LIVE animal in your
       inventory. A Bug Net speeds the wheel (see WORK WHEEL). This applies to
       every catchable animal including butterflies — they catch bare-handed and
       flee at 2 m/s like the rest.
     * TAME = feed the FAVOURITE food. Befriends the animal IN PLACE: it converts
       to a tame 'released_' pet (stays in the world, pettable / produces /
       cats follow) but does NOT enter your inventory. Favourites: chicken = any
       seed, cow = pairy, cat = milk OR any fish, dog = meat.
     * PLANT PRODUCE on chicken/cow → FEED for produce: consume the plant, get
       1 egg (chicken) / 1 milk (cow). 1-hour per-animal cooldown. A recently
       PETTED animal has a 50% chance to double the yield.
     * any other food → YUCK (wasted bite, no result).
 - PESTS — crow / deer / slime = DEFEAT (NOT caught). Finishing removes the
   creature and drops its product (crow → feather, deer → meat; slimes drop
   nothing). They do NOT flee during the wheel. The two halves differ:
     * GAME (crow / deer) — a HUNT. Tap to start the timed work wheel; the BUG
       NET speeds it by tier (see WORK WHEEL), the same slot the catch wheel
       uses — one tool takes animals, and weapons are for enemies. Game is not
       an enemy: nothing auto-fires at it and no shot can hit it, so a hunt is
       always something you chose.
     * ENEMIES (slime, and every cave monster) — see COMBAT. Tapping one starts
       the HP-driven combat wheel instead, and a sword starts it for you.
 - RELEASE: select a caught animal and tap a passable cell to place it (chickens
   release as a flock of 4; others one at a time). Released animals persist in
   save.released and re-spawn at their spot.
 - PET: tap a released ("tame") animal with anything (or nothing) for a happy
   sound + a 10-minute petting boost (the +50% double-yield above). Petting a
   cat also makes it follow you for 5 minutes.
 - SCARECROW: a placeable item — wild crows and deer steer around it (4-cell
   aversion). Pick it back up any time.
 - CROWS additionally try to destroy nearby crops over time — scarecrows and
   defeating them are the counters.
 - SLIMES are an energy-leeching PEST (50/tile, every natural biome) — except
   within 20 cells of the starting anchor during a save's FIRST DAY, which is
   kept clear of them (see ONBOARDING). They ooze
   in slow hopping drifts and are lazily drawn toward the player (~half their
   hops amble your way). A slime within 1 cell (~5m, adjacent) drains 3 energy
   per second, surfaced as one throttled "🟢 slime drained N⚡" flash (see the
   fauna section). Not catchable or feedable, but an ENEMY (see COMBAT) — so
   they can be shot, auto-engaged and defeated, or just walked away from.

================================================================================
COMBAT
================================================================================
 - ENEMIES are the things that attack YOU: the wild SLIME on the surface and
   every cave MONSTER underground. Crow and deer are GAME, not enemies — see
   CREATURES. A slime tamed with a sapphire is a pet and is never a target.
 - HIT POINTS. Every enemy has HP (monster table; surface slime 15). Damage
   from any source drains the one pool, and an enemy left alone heals back to
   full after 20 minutes without a hit. HP is in-memory: a foe you softened up
   is whole again next session.
 - ONLY ONE WEAPON FIGHTS AT A TIME. `save.activeWeapon` (sword/bow/staff/null)
   picks which owned weapon auto-engages or auto-fires; the others sit inert —
   no auto-engage, no auto-fire — until switched to. Default is the newest
   weapon OBTAINED or UPGRADED (equipping one always makes it active); the
   player can also switch by tapping any owned weapon in the Relics inventory
   tab, which marks it with a small "E" (equipped/active) badge alongside its
   tier badge. This only gates the AUTOMATIC behaviours below — a bare-handed
   or manual tap-to-fight (tapping an enemy directly) and the crow/deer HUNT
   wheel are unaffected; the hunt wheel is the BUG NET's, not a weapon's, so
   which weapon is active never touches it.
 - THE THREE WEAPONS DO DIFFERENT THINGS, AND BOW/STAFF DIFFER IN KIND.
     sword  — MELEE. Drains HP while the combat wheel runs, at
              15/durationSeconds HP per second off the WORK WHEEL ladder (bare
              hands = the 9s tier-0 rung, so you can always fight). Being the
              ACTIVE weapon also AUTO-ENGAGES: the nearest enemy in reach is
              picked up without a tap. An auto-engaged fight is deliberately
              weightless — it does not swallow taps, root the character, or
              block the walk home, and it breaks off silently when the foe
              leaves reach.
     bow    — RANGED, an ARROW. While active, and at least one enemy is ON
              SCREEN, it looses one shot every 2 seconds along the COMPASS
              heading (4.5 cells/s, 8-cell range). Shots do NOT home and do
              NOT pick a target: you aim by turning. An arrow STOPS in the
              first foe it hits, and at anything solid on the way — cave rock
              underground (the same collision the body walks into), and on
              the surface a standing tree/bush or an unbroken mineral rock.
              Free to fire — no energy cost.
     staff  — RANGED, a PIERCING BOLT: same heading rules as the bow (2.0
              cells/s, 7-cell range), but a bolt ignores the world entirely
              (goes through rock and timber alike) and damages EVERY foe on
              its line once each, not just the first. Costs 1 ENERGY per bolt
              (silently skipped if you can't afford it — an auto-firing weapon
              must never spam "too tired"), and lands DOUBLE an arrow's damage
              PER SECOND: the energy is the price of the pierce and the punch.
              It fires on its OWN, SLOWER BEAT — every 4 s against the bow's
              2 s (Combat.SHOT[slot].fireIntervalMs, STAFF_BEAT_MUL = 2). That
              is pacing, not a nerf: shotDamage prices each shot at its own
              slot's interval, so one bolt simply carries four arrows' worth
              and the delivered rate is unchanged. NEVER halve a cadence
              without letting shotDamage see it, or the weapon quietly loses
              half its damage.
   A shot's damage is its tier's FULL melee-equivalent rate (bow) or DOUBLE
   that (staff) — the same rate a sword of that tier deals, or twice it — with
   no split across ranged slots: because only one weapon can ever be in play,
   there is nothing to split against (bow and staff used to fire simultaneously
   and stack, which is what the old split priced against; the doubling that
   remains is a deliberate difference in KIND, not a stacking guard). Bow and
   staff never speed the melee wheel, active or not; the shots are what they
   contribute. (No weapon touches the crow/deer HUNT wheel any more — that is
   the BUG NET's, see FAUNA.)
 - THE COMBAT WHEEL IS A HEALTH BAR. The arc is the foe's REMAINING HP, tinted
   green > amber > red, and there is no time limit — the fight ends when the HP
   does. An enemy hurt in the last 4 seconds also carries a small floating
   health ring of its own, so a shot from across the screen reports its damage
   the same way a sword swing does. Both rings sit on the kind's CROWN (the
   creature-wheel rule — see CLAUDE.md).
 - Dragon Powder doubles damage for its minute — melee and shots alike.
 - DEFEATING AN ENEMY PAYS GOLD — every enemy, not just the cave monsters. The
   bounty is derived from the kind's HP (a coin per 5 HP, floored at 1) plus a
   coin per 3 levels of depth, so a tougher foe can never pay less than an
   easier one and a hostile kind is priced the moment it has stats: purple
   slime $1, surface / cave slime $3, goblin archer $4, goblin $5. "Enemy" is
   Combat.isEnemyKind — the things that attack you — which is what puts the
   SURFACE SLIME on the wage; it fights you and eats your crops, and for a long
   time killing one paid nothing. Crow and deer are game, not enemies: they pay
   in feathers and meat. On top of the wage, one in ten CAVE MONSTERS also drops
   a buried-treasure roll (a hoard is something you turn up underground). A kill
   also ticks any kill quest and flashes. Combat costs no energy — sword,
   bare hands, and the bow are free; your time and your aim are the price.
   The one exception is the staff, which draws 1 energy per bolt for its
   pierce + double damage (see above).
 - EVERY route to a dead creature pays the same, because they all funnel through
   resolveDefeat — the tap-hunt wheel, the combat wheel, a killing bow/staff
   shot, and a PET's kill. That last one used to carry its own copy of the
   payout, so a dog finishing off a slime skipped the bounty, the quest tick,
   the treasure roll and the shiny fanfare.

================================================================================
CHESTS (POI-driven)
================================================================================
 - useful real-world POIs (shops, food, civic, healthcare, parks, sports,
   culture, street furniture, …) each spawn a generic chest sprite at the POI.
   Chests snap to the cell grid and dedupe by name within ~80m (per tile) /
   ~120m (cross-tile). Density is data-dependent (~tens per tile in town).
 - tap a chest within reach to open it for loot driven by the POI class.
   Tier yields T1=10 / T2=5 / T3=2 per drop (low-tier street furniture is
   reduced to 3/2/1). Category weightings (T1/T2/T3), abbreviated:
     food → produce 60/30/10   commerce → seeds 70/25/5   farm → seeds 40/40/20 (+1)
     flora → flower seeds 10/30/60   civic → 30/40/30   health → 50/30/20
     park → 40/40/20   lowtier (bus/fuel/lodging/gate) → 90/8/2, reduced yields
 - chest TIER shown as a coloured diamond above the sprite: T1 bronze (lowtier),
   T2 silver (commerce/park), T3 gold (food/health/civic/farm), T4 cyan (flora).
 - some opens roll a RELIC or ARMOR instead of normal loot — the chance varies
   by POI class, roughly 3.75% (lowtier/commerce/farm) up to 15% (civic/flora),
   biased by chest tier (gear reward handled per GEAR; both show a reward modal).
 - BAG-FULL: if loot won't fully fit you choose "Take what fits" (chest emptied)
   or "Leave for later" (chest kept; it remembers its exact roll in
   save.chestHold so you can't re-roll for something better).
 - opened chests stay open + dimmed and persist (save.opened).
 - every UNOPENED POI pings a slow, subtle ring around it — a thick outline
   that expands outward and fades, not a filled disc (~4.5 s per pulse,
   phase hashed off the POI id so a street doesn't throb in unison) —
   ambience that marks where the places are. Deliberately exempt from the
   out-of-reach dim that darkens other ground decoration (tools/layer_audit.js
   pins this) so it stays legible across the whole visible map, not just the
   small radius around the player.
 - every POI's concrete pad (and its mini variant, see below) is tinted an
   electric light blue — punchier than the rest of the blue-white treasure
   family — since the pad is the one surface that has to carry "a place
   lives here" on its own, with no gem or halo riding along.
 - minor/lowtier POIs (bus stops, ATMs, fuel, intersections, etc.) get a MINI
   version of the same pad — smaller and dimmer — instead of no pad at all;
   only genuine loose starter supply crates (no poiClass) render with no pad.
 - POI names are RUSTICIFIED for display (Hospital→Apothecary, School→Hedge
   School, Coffee→Roastery, …) and shown as a label above the chest + in the
   loot flash, tinted the same subtle blue as every other POI name — including
   minor/lowtier POIs; only loose supply crate labels stay plain white.

================================================================================
COIN-BURST POIs (ATM / bicycle parking)
================================================================================
 - ATM and bicycle_parking POIs render as tinted chests but DON'T give chest
   loot. Tapping scatters 8-12 individual coin pickups (+$1 each) on walkable
   cells within ~25m; each coin expires after 60s.
 - one burst per POI per UTC day (save.coinBurstClaimed); coins are in-memory
   only (not persisted). A second tap the same day flashes "already used today".

================================================================================
TREASURE MARKS (X on the ground)
================================================================================
 - claim by tapping the cell the X is drawn on (and within reach). Reward via the unified
   rarity picker — mostly a small common (≈$1 or a common seed), sometimes an
   uncommon, rarely a rare. Found state persists in save.foundTreasures.
 - sources: the STARTER TILE seeds a trail of 4 fixed "starter crates" — real
   kind:'chest' objects carrying a `fixedLoot` payload (9 each of potato seed,
   rockfruit seed, rockfruit and wood — that ORDER, nearest crate first, is the
   ladder's order of need), opened via the standard chest modal but with no
   rarity roll — laid along the route to the relic chest a
   screen out (see ONBOARDING), and clears a tutorial pocket of
   obstacles; every PARKING lot spawns one guaranteed X; non-starter tiles add a
   few random X marks plus extra X's beside FOOTPATHS (terrain 8 — the spawner
   samples path cells, and never knew or needed a way's name). Residential X's only
   spawn near a road.

================================================================================
STREET RESTORATION (the road band itself)
================================================================================
 - THE ROAD BAND IS DILAPIDATED BY DEFAULT and the player rebuilds it. Every
   way road_overlay.js strokes is painted cracked, damp and missing setts (the
   weathering tile on the base canvas); wherever a stretch has sat inside the
   player's lit reach CONTINUOUSLY for PATH_STONE_DWELL_MS (2000 ms, app.js) it
   turns to clean near-black cobble with a hairline kerb, forever, saved.
   Footpaths restore too, to a darker packed earth. Rail never.
 - A STRETCH IS METRES, NOT CELLS. What restores is an INTERVAL OF ARCLENGTH
   along ONE LINE of one OSM way, in tile-local metres — the same coordinate
   the band is stroked in. The terrain grid under-reports a road every time (a
   way rasterizes exactly one cell wide however wide it is; parking aisles
   rasterize to no cell at all), so a per-cell rule could never line up with
   what the player sees restored. Until Sep 2026 this was LIT PEBBLES — one
   sprite per 20 m of path, thinned by the renderer — and half a street
   restored between two of them paid nothing at all. The pebbles, their lit
   textures, their light row and the 'cobble' spritesheet are all gone.
 - A STREET PIECE IS ONE LINE OF ONE FEATURE, never the feature. About a
   quarter of the transportation features on a real tile are merged multi-lines
   (one had 42 disconnected lines over 6.8 km), so keying on the feature id
   alone would weld unrelated streets into one row. Streets.lineKey hashes the
   LINE — its class, its two ends and its vertex count — beside the id, and the
   hash is stable forever because the save is keyed on it.
 - ONLY THE TILE SQUARE COUNTS. MVT geometry runs past [0, extent] into the
   tile's buffer and the same metres come back inside the NEIGHBOUR tile's copy
   of the way, so Streets.tileSpans clips to the square and the sweep
   intersects with it. Without that every way crossing a tile edge would be
   restored and paid for twice.
 - THE SWEEP (app.js _sweepStreets, called from update()) has two halves.
     * THE SCAN (_rescanStreets) is memoised on the reach CELL plus the rounded
       radius: standing still can't bring fresh street into the bubble, and the
       only things that can are moving to another cell or the reach changing.
       It walks the 3×3 tiles' transportation layers, skips rail ('rail' /
       'transit') and KEEPS everything else (parking aisles included — the
       overlay draws them), bbox-prefilters each line against the reach circle
       in tile-local metres, then takes Streets.reachIntervals (an EXACT grid
       traversal per segment, never sampling) ∩ tileSpans − what is already
       restored, and pushes it as a SNAPSHOT onto the dwell sight.
       The reach gate is coords.js cellInReach measured from the PLAYER'S REACH
       CELL, never the camera anchor — a peek drag restores nothing extra.
     * THE RIPEN (_ripenStreets) runs EVERY frame, because the thing it waits
       on is the clock, not the player. Streets.createSight().ripeAll returns
       what has been in sight for every instant of the last dwell (the
       intersection of every snapshot in the window plus the one current when
       it opened); Streets.restore unions it into the save and reports only the
       metres that were NEW.
   Sight is CONTINUOUS: an empty snapshot drops a key's whole history, so
   clipping the edge of the bubble in passing restarts the clock. The auto-walk
   home (_driftingHome), a cave (depth > 0) and a flat battery (reachM <= 0)
   all clear the sight and bank nothing — that is the game moving the body, not
   the player looking at anything.
 - THE SAVE:
     save.streets      = { "<z/tx/ty>": { "<lineKey>": [s0,s1, s0,s1, …] } }
     save.streetsEpoch = n     ← bumped by any restore that added metres
     save.trail        = { metres, prizes }
   Flat pairs because a walked city keeps one row per line of every way the
   player has stood beside. The epoch is folded into road_overlay.js's rebuild
   key, so a restore repaints the restored canvas on the next frame and nothing
   repaints on the frames between. savemigrate.js folds the old
   save.trail.stones into metres (× 20) and drops save.pathStones outright —
   lit pebble CELLS do not map onto intervals; prizes and progress are kept.
 - THREE PASSES DRAW IT. Both baked canvases live in roadGeomContainer and
   rebuild together, keyed on the snapped anchor cell + tile readiness + epoch:
     * 'roadgeom_overlay' — the dilapidated network, at image alpha 0.61.
     * 'roadgeom_restored' — the rebuilt stretches, stroked from the save's
       intervals through Streets.subLineM at the way's own widthPxFor, above
       the base at RESTORED_ALPHA (0.92), with the clean sett pattern and the
       kerb. No edge nibble: a clean edge is the point.
     * RoadOverlay.drawLive(scene, runs) — a plain Graphics above both,
       cleared and re-stroked EVERY frame from app.js _drawStreetLive. It
       carries the DWELL PREVIEW (the in-sight-unrestored intervals, alpha
       ramping to STREET_PREVIEW_ALPHA over the dwell, so the two seconds read
       as a thing being done) and the SHINE (a white run down a stretch the
       instant it comes back, fading over STREET_SHINE_MS = Lighting.BLAST_MS).
       Points are projected through scene.worldMetersToScreen — the
       camera-anchored projection — with the container's own sub-cell offset
       subtracted back out. The sub-polylines are cut ONCE per scan, not per
       frame: a merged way can carry thousands of vertices.
 - THE BLAST (restoration fanfare) is ONE thing at any size — app.js
   _blastAt(wmx, wmy, { radiusCells, ringPx, chips, sparks, colour, material }),
   fired at the instant a thing comes back. ONE per sweep, at the MIDPOINT OF
   THE LONGEST piece the step restored (the stretch the player is looking at,
   not a metre of driveway at the rim). Three parts:
     * THE LIGHT. Lighting.blast puts a transient entry on scene._blasts —
       world metres, its own radius, colour and duration — and the lightmap's
       collectBlasts converts it against THIS frame's camera anchor, stamps it
       with alpha (1-t)² and scale FLASH_SCALE_FROM→1, and drops it when its
       time is up. Stored in world metres precisely so a peek drag leaves the
       flash on the ground it went off on. Defaults are the old per-pebble
       numbers exactly: 2.5 cells (BLAST_STONE_R_CELLS), 900 ms.
     * THE CHIPS. Debris in the thing's own material (particles.js 'stone' for
       a street, 'timber' for a building; `material` recolours a preset, which
       bakes its own texture and emitter — never a tint).
     * THE SPARKS. A ring of stars in the moment's colour ('trailspark' in the
       street ink for a street, 'buildspark' in UI_GREEN for a building).
   `ringPx` is what scales the particles to the thing: 0 throws them out of one
   point (a street stretch), a building's half-extent throws them off its WALLS
   and scales the count with the radius (particles.js ringPoints / burstCount,
   capped at BURST_MAX). Two call sites today — every restoring sweep, and
   every wreck restored (at the footprint's centre, radius = its half-diagonal
   + BLAST_HOUSE_PAD_CELLS, ring = its half-extent), the latter fired the
   moment the role is frozen and banked, before the Restored! card opens over
   it. Add a third by calling _blastAt; never by adding a light row.

 - ONE LADDER FOR THE WHOLE WORLD. Every metre restored anywhere adds to a
   single running total (save.trail = { metres, prizes }); the first treasure
   lands at Trail.GOAL_STEP_M (200 m), the next at 400, the next at 600, and so
   on — a couple of hundred metres for the first prize and a proper expedition
   for the tenth. A sweep that crosses more than one goal at once queues the
   ceremonies rather than stacking modals. The arithmetic lives in src/trail.js
   and the interval algebra in src/streets.js, so the headless suite pins both.
 - THE PRIZES USED TO BE PER PATH: each named way on each tile ran its own
   counter, its own 20-stone segment with a short remainder at the end, its own
   "under 8 cells pays nothing" floor and its own row in the save, and worldgen
   ran a wavefront pass over every tile to decide which stone belonged to which
   way. The same walk therefore paid differently depending on how OSM happened
   to split the ways under it. None of that survives — no names, no segments,
   no per-path anything. A metre is a metre wherever it is restored.
 - each sweep pops a small COUNTER in UI_STREET_INK — metres banked over the
   current goal ("137/200 m", Trail.label, the ONE formatter) — ON the stretch
   it just rebuilt, seated through the projection (_worldToastAt) so a peek
   drag keeps it on its street. One counter per sweep, however much street the
   step brought back, throttled to one per STREET_COUNTER_MIN_MS (1000 ms)
   because a walk restores metres on nearly every frame; the ladder banks
   regardless. On the sweep that PAYS it jumps the throttle and reads the goal
   just completed, full ("200/200 m", Trail.readout), never the carried
   remainder against the next rung — the ladder grows, and "60/400 m" beside a
   prize that paid at 200 read as a disagreement. The prize modal's header is
   "Thou hast traveled far"; the walk it paid at is the pick's flavour line,
   printed through the same Trail.label.
 - each prize is a lowtier-chest roll at tier 4 through the unified rarity
   picker, shown with the chest reward fanfare, AND IT IS A CHOICE: two rolls
   are offered and the player keeps one (Trail.PRIZE_CHOICES). The options must
   differ — gold is gold and the same item at another quantity is the same card
   — and nothing is granted until a button is pressed, so the roll turned down
   was never theirs. The roll also IMPROVES as the walks get longer:
   Trail.rollBonusFor spends one extra boost-chain step per prize already won,
   capped (past the cap a chain step only buys consolation coins).
 - WHICH CELLS ARE PATH AT ALL is still geometric, and still matters — the PATH
   terrain code decides ground colour, tillability and where the X-mark spawner
   samples, even though nothing draws a pebble on it any more. A cell becomes
   PATH only where at least one full cell width of way lies inside it — the
   path runs THROUGH the cell rather than clipping its corner or stopping just
   inside. Measured by worldgen's accumulateLineSpan (an exact grid traversal,
   not sampling: a straight orthogonal crossing measures exactly 1.0, a
   corner-to-corner diagonal sqrt(2)) in a PRE-PASS over every footpath before
   any painting, since a cell's total is the SUM over all path ways touching it
   — so a junction where two ways each cut a corner still qualifies. A clipped
   cell keeps its own biome and stays tillable, spawnable and unclaimable,
   because there is no path there.
   ONE EXEMPTION: the connectivity elbow. forEachLineCell stamps an extra cell
   on each diagonal step so a width-1 line stays 4-connected (the renderer
   draws orthogonal arms only). Its span is ~0 by construction, so gating it
   deletes it — a 45-degree footpath measured 31 orphaned cells of 65. It sits
   between two cells the line genuinely crosses and is always painted.
 - AND A RUN OF UNDER 15 PATH CELLS IS NOT A PATH (WorldGen.MIN_PATH_RUN_CELLS,
   ~105 m). OSM leaves footway scraps everywhere — a driveway apron, a crossing,
   the tail of a way — and each landed as a lone patch of path in a field. The
   whole run dissolves back to the biome it covered. A run touching the tile
   edge is exempt: it continues into the neighbour tile, which counts only its
   own cells.

WELLS / FOUNTAINS

 - OSM fountains become a 'well' object: a water source on dry land. It used to

================================================================================
ECONOMY (buying & selling)
================================================================================
 - currency = coins (save.money). Start with $25. Earn by selling, picking up
   coins, treasure/chests, and coin bursts.
 - WRECK-HOUSE RESTORATION: every small house starts as a derelict WRECK with no
   shop function (the starter trailer is exempt — Home works from day one). Tap a
   wreck to restore it for a one-time payment of 3 rockfruit (stone). Restoring
   reveals the house's ROLE, which is frozen into save.restoredHouses[id] so it
   never shifts on later loads.
 - SHOP ROLES are assigned by RESTORE ORDER, not address, for the opening run:
   PRESEED_RESTORE_ROLES makes the first restores a fixed tutorial sequence —
   0 = BLACKSMITH, 1 = TRADER, 2 = plain, 3 = MARKET, 4-5 = plain, and the 15th
   (order 14) = WIZARD TOWER. Restores BEYOND those slots fall back to the
   address-derived role (shops.js Shops.shopType): ending 9 = BLACKSMITH (forges
   gear from bars/gems), 2/6 = MARKET (cash-only produce stock), 1/8 = TRADER
   (barter-only), else PLAIN. The first restored blacksmith is stamped the
   starter forge (save.starterBlacksmithId); the first market vends only T1/T2
   seeds for cash. PLAIN houses run "delivery" deals (see DELIVERIES below).
   (These are BUY/trade specialties — selling is home-only, see SELL below.)
 - FORTS (BUILDING_MED) start SEALED; pay a one-time stack of wood (tracked in
   save.unlockedForts) to open the quartermaster — then up to 5 deals/hour. The
   wood price FOLLOWS A PROGRESSION across forts: FORT_UNLOCK_WOOD_START (6) for
   the first fort, +FORT_UNLOCK_WOOD_STEP (6) per fort already unsealed — 6, 12,
   18, 24, 30 … — capped at FORT_UNLOCK_WOOD (30). CASTLES/TOWERS
   (BUILDING_LARGE) unsealed but not yet CLAIMED (opened only via the delivery
   gate below, quest unsolved): never run out, always sell RELICS (at 4× base,
   minus bow discount), and are never "busy". A CLAIMED castle (the player
   solved its quest board there) no longer trades relics — instead its
   castellan greets the player ("Thank you for visiting us, my lord.") and
   offers ONE daily favour, gated per castle per UTC day
   (save.castleServiceClaimed): REST for CASTLE_REST_FRAC (10%) of the energy
   bar, or COLLECT CASTLE_TAX_GOLD ($10) in gold — see presentCastleServiceOffer
   / _castleRest / _castleTax in app.js. Plain houses do 1 deal/hour. Hour
   buckets are per-house.
 - CASTLE DELIVERY GATE: every castle begins sealed by corrupt post-apocalyptic
   residents and stays shut until you've proven yourself on the delivery routes —
   completed LIFETIME deliveries (save.deliveryCount). The gate also FOLLOWS A
   PROGRESSION across castles: CASTLE_DELIVERY_GATE_START (2) deliveries for the
   first castle you open, +CASTLE_DELIVERY_GATE_STEP (1) per castle already
   opened — 2, 3, 4, 5 … — capped at CASTLE_DELIVERY_GATE (5). Opened castles
   are tracked in save.openedCastles. The price of entry is footwork, not goods.
   (This REPLACED the old one-time tier-2 goods tribute; once the tally clears a
   castle's gate, that castle trades normally forever.)
 - WIZARD TOWER (the 15th restored wreck): sells a LADDER of upgrades for
   DISCOVERY points (see SHINY VARIANTS) — WIZARD_UPGRADE_COST (5) Discovery a
   rung, no delivery gate. One rung per visit, climbed strictly in order, and
   the tower always offers the first rung not yet finished (app.js
   wizardLadder / wizardNextRung):
     1. INNER LIGHT  ×6 — +0.5 cell of reach each (see MOVEMENT & REACH).
     2. FULL MEASURE ×3 — QUANTITY luck: P(a find comes in a bigger stack),
        linear onto RARITY_TUNING.qtyLuckMaxP (0.35). This was the AMULET's
        until Sep 2026 (0.05 per amulet tier, so a Frost amulet sat at exactly
        0.35); the ceiling is unchanged, so the amulet lost a bonus rather than
        the player. save.qtyUpgrades.
     3. KEEN EYE     ×7 — the RING, i.e. TIER luck: P(a find comes a tier
        rarer), rarity.js ringLuck at 0.01 a tier. Until Sep 2026 the Ring rode
        along with the Inner Light — one purchase bought both — so the two
        kinds of luck are two things you buy now.
   The Ring is still the wizard tower's exclusive gift: it is NEVER sold or
   forged anywhere else (excluded from buildRelicOffer), so the tower is the
   only way to one.
   The percentage a rung's modal prints comes from rarity.js's own qtyLuck, not
   a second formula, so a rung can't advertise a number the loot roll won't pay.
 - OFFER MODAL: shows the item (sprite + name ×qty), a cost, and Buy/Cancel
   (greyed if you can't afford). Castles/forts may show a re-roll button
   (cost $5 × 2^rerolls).
 - BUY cost generation: cash storefronts (markets + generic houses) always
   price in CASH (1.2-3.0× the item value, shrunk by your Bow tier). BARTER is
   exclusively the TRADER shop kind's job (its own qty-scaled deal + re-roll —
   see the shop roles above). The old mixed "1/3 cash / 2/3 barter" roll was
   removed so the two idioms map cleanly onto shop type.
 - QUANTITY: cash purchases hand over exactly ONE unit (low-tier seeds add the
   bulk-pack bonus); the ×TRADE_OFFER_QTY (2) bundle is BARTER-ONLY, so cash
   buys can never be resold at a profit. The seed rotation (BUY_LIST) covers
   T1-T3 seeds only — magical flower seeds (sunflower/fireflower/iceflower)
   are loot finds (flora chests, rare trader/fort rarity rolls), never on the
   shop rotation.
 - SELL: selling happens only at your HOME shop. With a stack selected, tapping
   home sells one at PRICES[id] × sword multiplier (0.5× with no sword → 1.0× at
   Frost). Other houses are buy/trade-only.
 - the Bow relic shrinks the buy markup (T7 = buy at par). The Staff is a pure
   combat weapon — it no longer affects prices.
 - ROADSIDE STANDS (the coffee cart, the fruit stall, the fishmonger — anything
   loot.js produceStandFor matches) sell BELOW the listed price, not at par and
   never at the 1.2-3.0x village-shop markup: a stand is a producer selling its
   own goods, not a shop restocking from a wholesaler.
   The discount has a hard ceiling — A STAND MUST NEVER BE AN ARBITRAGE PUMP.
   Because the sell side is player-scaled (sword 0.5x -> 1.0x), a flat discount
   would become free money the moment a player carried a tier-4 sword: buy at
   0.75, sell at 0.79, repeat. So the stand price tracks the player's OWN sell
   multiplier and stays above it, capped at par:
       standMul = min(1, max(0.75, sellMultiplier + 0.05))
       no sword (sell 0.50) -> pay 0.75   coffee $40 -> $30
       sword T4 (sell 0.79) -> pay 0.84   coffee $40 -> $34
       sword T7 (sell 1.00) -> pay 1.00   coffee $40 -> $40, break-even
   What guarantees the invariant is the TRACKING, not the 0.05 margin — that is
   headroom so a later tweak to either curve doesn't land on the line. The price
   is ceil()ed so rounding always favours the stand. The modal shows the saving
   ("save $10"), suppressed once the price is back at par.
   ShopsMath.standPrice owns this; shops_math.test.js sweeps every item price
   and every sword tier, plus $1-$500, asserting resale never exceeds the price
   paid.
 - DELIVERIES: a PLAIN house wants a SET of 2-3 specific produce. Turn in a
   complete set (a quantity stepper batches multiple sets) and it pays the set's
   summed full price × DELIVERY_BONUS_MULT (1.5) — a 50% premium over selling the
   items one by one. A delivered house is satisfied for the rest of the UTC day,
   then refreshes. The wanted tier RAMPS with your lifetime tally: it biases from
   PRODUCE_TIER_MIN (1) toward PRODUCE_TIER_MAX (7), reaching the cap at
   DELIVERY_TIER_CAP (100) deliveries — early neighbours ask for potatoes and
   berries, a veteran courier for coconuts and goldenfish. The first 3 delivery
   houses are pinned to tier 1 and the 4th wants foraged flowers, to ease the
   start. Each completed set bumps save.deliveryCount — the same lifetime tally
   that unseals castles (5) and ramps the delivery produce tier.

================================================================================
GEAR: RELICS & ARMOR
================================================================================
 - 7 MATERIAL TIERS for all gear: Wood, Copper, Iron, Gold, Platinum, Crimson,
   Frost (cost + effect climb together). One relic per slot, one armor per slot;
   buying/forging an equal-or-lower tier into an occupied slot is refused.
 - GEAR REWARDS (a relic/armor rolled from ANY source — chest, fishing, …): an
   upgrade auto-equips; a non-upgrade is discarded (cashed out). Always shown in
   a reward modal.
 - RELIC SLOTS and effects (items.js RELIC_DEFS):
     pick   — lets you break rocks; tier shortens the wheel + cuts energy
     axe    — lets you chop trees; tier shortens the wheel + cuts energy, and
              raises the ACORN drop on a fell (10% → 100% at Frost, geometric,
              see CHOPPING)
     hoe    — cheaper tilling, per-tier chance of free, AND sets the tilled
              cell's produce QUALITY (see FARMING)
     can    — a watering can spring the crop a growth stage (tier/7 chance)
     rod    — fish from water; tier raises catch + rare odds
     bugnet — THE animal tool: speeds the catch wheel AND the crow/deer hunt
              wheel by tier (required for neither; both work bare-handed at
              the tier-0 rung, only slowly enough that a quick animal usually
              gets away)
     bags   — raises the per-item stack cap (9 with none → 249 at T7)
     ring   — TIER luck: +1%/tier chance to bump chest loot up a tier; granted
              ONLY by the wizard tower's Keen Eye rung (see ECONOMY)
     amulet — powers STICK WALKING (see below); higher tier = faster + cheaper.
              It carried the QUANTITY luck too until Sep 2026; that is the
              wizard's Full Measure rung now.
     sword  — MELEE weapon (drains an enemy's HP on the combat wheel, and
              auto-engages the nearest enemy in reach WHEN ACTIVE — see
              COMBAT); raises sell prices (0.5× → 1.0× at Frost) whether
              active or not
     bow    — RANGED weapon, an arrow (auto-fires along the compass every 2 s
              while an enemy is on screen, WHEN ACTIVE; stops in the first
              foe hit and at solid ground); lowers buy prices (T7 = par)
              whether active or not
     staff  — RANGED weapon, a piercing bolt (as the bow, shorter reach, but
              ignores the world and hits every foe on its line; costs 1
              energy/bolt, fires every 4 s against the bow's 2 s, and lands
              double an arrow's damage per second); no price effect
     (only ONE of the three auto-fights at a time — save.activeWeapon, see
     COMBAT. None of them touches the crow/deer HUNT wheel: that is the bug
     net's — see FAUNA.)
 - ARMOR SLOTS (helmet / chest / legs / boots) only raise MAX ENERGY per tier.
 - STICK WALKING (amulet): the movement stick is ALWAYS on screen — no relic
   needed and no separate mode. It walks the character somewhere OTHER than
   the GPS target, and what it walks is the body itself: there is no ghost and
   no snap-back on release. The distance walked is banked as an offset from
   your real position, so each new fix targets (GPS + offset) and the ground
   you covered by hand survives it; a GPS jump big enough to be a relocation
   (GPS_SNAP_M, 200 m) re-anchors you and drops the offset — the body is
   placed on the fix outright rather than walking there. Surface only:
   underground a snap would drop you inside solid rock, so down there the body
   always mines its way over, however far it is. Walking WITH the GPS is
   free — that's you actually walking. Stick walking costs energy per cell,
   and the AMULET is the upgrade to it: speed 5× walk bare (≈ one cell per
   second — real walk pace is unusably slow on an 11-cell view) → 15.5× at Frost,
   cost 1/cell bare → ~0.15/cell at Frost. At 0 energy the stick stops
   ("too tired"); the GPS still moves you.
   Straying is shaped by a 3-cell ring around your real position: INSIDE it
   stick walking costs 80% less (pottering about where you're actually
   standing is nearly free), OUTSIDE it you pay full price and the character
   TINTS DARKER the further out you go, with a black pulsing halo behind. At
   0 energy the character tints RED with a red pulsing halo instead (that
   warning wins when both apply). Let the stick sit idle for WALK_HOME_IDLE_MS
   (5000 ms) and the character WALKS ITSELF HOME — the offset bleeds back to
   zero at stick pace, free of charge; any push restarts the timer. Surface
   only, and never while a work wheel is running (standing still to chop a tree
   is being busy, not idle).
   PAST GPS_SNAP_M (200 m) THE RETURN IS NOT WALKED — the body is placed on the
   fix outright, exactly as a jumped fix does it. The gap is the same gap
   whichever side opened it, so one constant decides both: past it, the
   distance is travel the player never made on foot, and the body chases at
   DEBUG_SPEED_MUL × walk pace (14 m/s) at best, so half a kilometre is the
   better part of a minute of watching a character walk in a straight line with
   the stick unusable under it. Measured body-to-fix, not by the offset — the
   offset bleeds at stick pace while the body chases at 14 m/s, so it can reach
   zero with the body still hundreds of metres behind, and that lag is the same
   walk from the player's side of the screen. The DEBOUNCE still owns when the
   return starts: distance decides how the return is made, never when — a warp
   that ignored the debounce would be the 500 ms hair-trigger below with a
   teleport on the end.
   That delay is a DEBOUNCE (so lifting a thumb to reposition it doesn't send
   the character trotting off), not a pause, and it has been wrong in both
   directions. At 3000 ms it measured as three full seconds of the character
   doing nothing after release — walk 43 m out, let go, and the offset sat
   frozen for 3.1 s before covering the whole way back in 2.9 s. Chasing that
   took it to 700 ms and then 500 ms, which is a hair-trigger: players nudge
   themselves along in short pushes, and any gap longer than the timer had the
   character walking BACK against them (with a 500 ms push / 900 ms pause
   pattern, 700 ms spent 23% of forward travel going backwards across 7
   direction reversals). 5000 ms is where playtesting settled — past the longest
   natural gap between two deliberate pushes, so the return only starts when
   the player has actually stopped.
   The timer decides only when the return BEGINS; it begins gently — the speed
   eases in over WALK_HOME_RAMP_MS (1100 ms, squared) instead of switching on at
   full pace. That ramp is what keeps a generous debounce from being a cliff in
   the other direction: a push landing just past the timer gives up almost no
   ground, while a real stop is at full walking pace about a second later.
   The lead-line hint (WALK_HOME_HINT_IDLE_MS, 6500 ms) must stay LONGER than
   the walk's delay — it announces a walk, so it can't precede one — but not so
   much longer that an ordinary return finishes before it is due, which is what
   happened when the walk dropped to 700 ms and the hint stayed at 5000 ms; it
   tracks the debounce plus a beat and a half. walk_home.test.js pins both
   bounds. Two things borrow a tier rather
   than adding a mode: DRAGON POWDER walks as tier 8 for its minute (plus 2×
   damage and the dragon sprite) and the SPEED POTION as tier 9. The ☰ "Free
   walking (debug)" toggle drops the stamina cost to nothing and with it the
   0-energy stop; it changes nothing else.
 - TIER GATING: the only gate on rarer relic/armor tiers is the per-source LOOT
   RULE — each chest/fishing source has a tier ceiling derived from its own tier
   (rarity.js: maxTier / relicCap, plus the chest-tier→preferred clamp), so a
   bus chest still can't cough up a Frost relic. The old harvest/catch
   "milestone" unlocks (sunflower→Gold, cow→Platinum, …) were REMOVED — they
   were a second, invisible lock that duplicated the loot-rule ceiling.

CONSUMABLES

 - Self-targeted consumables (honey / book / potions / sapphire portal) are
   used via a dedicated USE button (Use / Read / Drink / Portal) that appears
   below the inventory bar while one is selected — same spot and behaviour as

   reports how many respond). Consumed on use — the animals eat it. (Was the
   Flute until Sep 2026; old saves fold `flute` into `honey` on load.)
 - BOOK: select it and tap Read — ~50% a play tip, ~50% a directional
   hint to the nearest unopened chest ("about N paces northwest"). Consumed.
 - CAMPFIRE: select a COAL and tap an empty bare (tillable) cell to light a fire
   (consumes the coal). A fire is a safe, slow recovery spot: it refills energy
   within 3 cells (full bar in 360s, see REST) and repels the surface slime
   within 4 cells, so it doubles as a perimeter ward in the wild. Placeable
   underground too, where it wards the cave's own entry-level monsters (cave
   slime, purple slime) the same way — but a goblin or its archer, from the
   2nd cave level down, is undeterred by firelight (see FIRE_WARD_MAX_DEPTH,
   app.js). One fire per cell; it persists in save.fires until you tap it to
   extinguish (no coal refund).

================================================================================
UNDERGROUND CAVES (Phase 1)
================================================================================
 - cave-rock clusters on the surface seed a "descend" STAIRCASE beside them (a
   mine mouth). Tapping a staircase within reach changes the player's DEPTH:
   0 = surface, 1, 2, … = cave levels below. Depth is persisted (a reload
   underground stays underground) and the world re-points WorldGen at it.
 - each cave level is generated as the "NEGATIVE" of the level directly above:
   every walkable surface cell becomes CAVE_FLOOR (walkable); every non-walkable
   cell (water, any road, any building) becomes CAVE_WALL — solid rock you can't
   pass. So surface buildings/roads/water "cast" obstructions underground. This
   recurses up to the surface, so depth N derives deterministically from N-1.
 - staircases mirror across levels at the SAME world point: a level's DOWN-stair
   becomes the next level's UP-stair (you arrive standing on the way back up),
   with a fresh DOWN-stair a few cells off so up/down aren't co-located.
 - tiles are cached PER DEPTH. Mineral rocks are the draw — but caves are no
   longer quiet: hostile WANDERING MONSTERS spawn on CAVE_FLOOR cells (no
   surface fauna underground). They're the slime mechanic, parametrised — each
   drifts toward the player and drains energy when within RANGE, differing by
   HP / RANGE / DAMAGE / SPEED, and is DEFEATED via the work wheel (HP scales
   the wheel; tougher kinds take longer; no drops). Each monster lands at most
   one hit every 2 s (MONSTER_HIT_MS). Kinds are gated by depth so descending
   introduces new foes; density rises gently with depth:
     * cave slime    (depth 1+) — 30 hp, range 1, 4⚡/hit, slow
     * bat           (depth 1+) — 12 hp, range 1, 2⚡/hit, fast + erratic, hovers
     * goblin        (depth 2+) — 50 hp, range 1, 8⚡/hit, medium speed (a slog)
     * goblin archer (depth 3+) — 36 hp, range 3, 6⚡/hit — the ONLY ranged foe
   THE FIRST SLIME IS THE TUTORIAL. Those numbers are the authored baseline
   DOUBLED (CAVE_ENEMY_MUL): the wild surface slime is the only enemy above
   ground and the first anyone meets, so it stays gentle, and every enemy
   beyond it — all of them underground, met by a player who went looking — has
   double the HP and does double the damage. Applied as one rule over the
   table, so a kind added to it inherits the doubling. Knock-ons are derived:
   double HP is exactly double the time to kill at any weapon tier (the dps
   identity is untouched), and the coin bounty pays per HP.
   The goblin archer needs a CLEAR LINE, the same as your own bow — it used to
   chip at you through solid rock from a tunnel you couldn't see.
   Placeholder art: every monster reuses the slime sprite with a per-kind tint
   (real sheets are a per-kind assets.js + render.js swap). Monsters can't be
   tamed (mango/sapphire don't apply); a defeated monster is recorded in
   save.caught so it stays dead across reloads, like surface fauna.

SAVED GAMES (slots)

UI COLOUR LANGUAGE
================================================================================
Two colours carry MEANING; everything else is neutral chrome. A player glancing
at any screen should be able to answer "is this something I press, or something
I just won?" without reading a word.

 - BLUE-WHITE = TREASURE & POWERUPS — what the WORLD gives the player.
     --treasure      #f4f8ff  near-white with a blue cast; surfaces + frames
     --treasure-ink  #cfe2ff  the same role as TEXT on a dark ground
     --treasure-deep #7fb0ff  saturated; glow, side faces, deep accents
   Worn by: the POI pads (the pale plinth under every chest), the POI halo
   breathing under them, the chest/treasure reward ceremony modal (frame,
   header, sparkle burst, primary button), and powerup grants (relics + armor
   equipped, Inner Light reach upgrades).

 - GOLD / YELLOW = PLAYER CONTROLS — what the PLAYER drives.
     --gold #ffe066, --gold-deep #ffd23a, --gold-dark #c8a64a, --gold-pale #fff3b0
   Worn by: buttons, the movement pad, inventory slots + tabs, HUD chrome and
   dialog frames, and money (coins are the currency the player spends, so they
   sit on the control side of the line).

 - EXCEPTIONS, and they are deliberate:
     * ITEM RARITY tier colours (loot.js) still colour a reward's NAME — rarity
       is information the ceremony's blue-white frame doesn't carry.
     * ENERGY keeps green → gold → red as it drains; DANGER keeps red. This is
       a deliberate exception to "gold = a control": the bar reports and cannot
       be pressed. It was briefly moved to amber to obey the law strictly, and
       moved back — the traffic-light reading is an idiom players already know,
       and a gauge carries no affordance for the gold to be confused with.
     * Ceremonies that are NOT loot reuse the reward modal with their own
       accent (passing out = orange, restoring a wreck / unsealing a fort =
       green) so a construction never reads as treasure.

 - ONE source of truth: the UI_* constants at the top of src/util.js (canvas
   text + Phaser graphics) mirrored as CSS custom properties in index.html
   (DOM). UI_CONTROL / UI_CONTROL_DIM name the control role; UI_TREASURE* name
   the treasure role. Reach for the ROLE that fits — never add a near-duplicate
   shade, and change both halves together.

FOG OF WAR
 - Land the player has NEVER WALKED is washed 80% black (render.js FOG_ALPHA).
   Walking reveals a disc of Fog.REVEAL_CELLS (3 cells, ~21 m) around the
   player PLUS two SWEEP ARMS — the row and column through the player, out to
   Fog.REVEAL_ARM_CELLS (6 cells) either side; what is revealed stays
   revealed, per save, forever.
 - The disc radius has a HARD CEILING at the viewport half-width. The view is
   VIEW_CELLS (11) wide, so the player sees 5 cells in every direction: reveal
   5 or more and every cell is revealed the instant it becomes visible — the
   fog would still be computed and still be stored and never once be seen. At
   3 (the reach radius: you reveal what you could have reached out and
   touched), fog.test.js pins the bound against the real VIEW_CELLS.
 - The SWEEP ARMS are pinned to EXACTLY one past the half-width (the view
   scrolls by sub-cell fractions, so the outermost partially-visible column
   sits a cell past it), and exist because the disc alone left a 2-cell band
   of fog rimming the view — and on a phone the view's left and right edges
   ARE the screen edges, so walking anywhere drew two near-black columns down
   both sides of the display (fog stacked on the distance falloff), which
   read as the app being letterboxed rather than as unexplored land. The arms
   are the look-down-the-cross-street reveal: the rank you are on — and every
   rank you have crossed — opens edge to edge, while land ahead of and
   diagonal to you keeps its fog, so the frontier still reads as a frontier
   and the screen never grows gutters.
 - The wash is PURE BLACK, not the biome-tinted `atmos.dim` the out-of-reach
   wash uses. That dim says "this biome, unlit"; fog says "you don't know", and
   colouring it would describe terrain the player hasn't seen. It STACKS with
   the distance falloff (0.90 at the viewport corner), so out at the corners
   fogged and explored ground are both near black and the fog edge reads
   mainly in the mid-field — retune the pair together, never the alpha alone.
 - The wash is A CONTINUOUS FIELD painted into a CANVAS TEXTURE, not Graphics
   fills. Darkness ramps with DISTANCE from the nearest revealed cell — 0.55
   one cell out, 0.70 two, FOG_ALPHA at three and past (FOG_RAMP_A), smoothstep
   between those samples — and the distance is measured to the EDGE of explored
   ground, half a cell in from the last explored cell's centre (FOG_EDGE_BIAS),
   so the feather lands on the unknown side and ground the player has walked
   stays legible. The frontier is then bent by two octaves of value noise keyed
   on ABSOLUTE world position, which is what makes it read as weather rather
   than as a vignette: a jitter keyed on the screen crawls along the boundary
   as the player walks and looks like the fog boiling, while world-keyed wisps
   put the same tongue of fog over the same ground every time you come back.
   The wisp push is TAPERED to nothing at BOTH ends of the ramp (4u(1-u)) —
   that taper is what keeps the two claims intact: walked ground takes no wisp
   at all, and the deep interior still lands on FOG_ALPHA exactly. Softening
   the edge must not lighten the unknown.
 - It was three concentric SHELLS of 32px rects until Sep 2026, with a hashed
   corner bite out of each shell's outermost cells. That softened the frontier
   without ever letting it stop being made of cells: the eye reads a 32px alpha
   step as a UI element laid on the world, and the bites only turned the
   staircase into a row of chamfered tiles. If you are tempted to draw the fog
   as rects again — at any resolution — that is the bug.
 - The field is computed at FOG_SUB (8) samples per cell and drawn up to
   CELL_PX with smoothing on; Phaser then blits the result 1:1, so the game's
   pixelArt (NEAREST) filtering cannot put the steps back. Bilinear, not the
   'high' resampler: the field is already smooth, and 'high' cost several times
   the milliseconds for under one alpha step of accuracy.
 - LAYER: fogContainer is LAST in the display list — above the ground, the
   lighting dim, the sprites, the rim haze, the distance falloff, the POI name
   tablets and the tier diamonds. Fog is the strongest claim of any darkening
   pass, and a label poking through it would name a shop the player has never
   found. (The player, the GPS ghost and the footprint trail set explicit
   depths and stay above it — they are the player's own kit, not world
   information.) Surface only: underground has the torch bubble instead, and
   a cave walk must not reveal the surface cells above it.
 - STORAGE is one BIT per cell, packed per tile (src/fog.js), zero-run coded
   and base64'd into save.fog = { w, tiles: { "tx/ty": blob } }. The Set-of-
   "ix_iy"-strings shape every other per-cell fact uses (tilled, picked,
   opened) is wrong here: those are dozens of cells, fog is thousands. Measured
   over a 5 km walk at a 228-cell tile edge (49°N): 2.1 KB of save across 4
   tiles, against 25.4 KB of raw bitsets and ~68 KB as a string Set that keeps
   growing per cell. A fully saturated tile is the bounded worst case at
   10.1 KB (base64 overhead on a mask the run coder can't help). save.fog
   records the width it was built at; a save from a different cells-per-tile is
   DROPPED rather than mis-indexed.
 - flush() caches the encoded blob per tile and drops it only when a bit is
   set, so the once-per-7 m save writes re-code the one or two tiles the player
   is standing across instead of all 64 loaded ones. A reload seeds that cache
   from the blobs it read, so the first flush of a session re-codes nothing.
 - The masks live in fog.js's own Map, deliberately NOT on the WorldGen tile
   cache entries beside `grid` / `roadMask`. Those are derived world state —
   loadTile LRU-prunes at 64 tiles and re-rasterises every 30 days, and either
   would silently wipe the player's exploration history.
 - COST: the reveal bails unless the player changed CELL (once per 7 m), and
   the renderer rebuilds only on that crossing or when Fog.revision moves —
   between crossings fogContainer just SCROLLS by the sub-cell fraction, the
   same trick the biome-seam layer uses. The fog bits are read in the ring scan
   that already resolves the terrain types, off the same cached tile run (~4
   mask lookups a frame, not 225). The repaint itself skips every lattice
   square whose four cells agree — all revealed, or all past the ramp —
   because bilinear interpolation between four equal corners IS that value
   and the wisp taper is zero at both ends, so the work tracks the LENGTH OF
   THE FRONTIER, not the area of the screen. That matters most while tiles
   are still landing, the one window where the pass runs every frame.
 - The ONBOARDING TRAIL is revealed when it is laid (_revealStarterTrail), and
   this is not optional. The starter trail is a SIGHTLINE CHAIN — walk to the
   crate you can see, and from there the next is in view, and the last puts the
   relic chest in view — and _placeStarterTrail seats it on the nearest road,
   BFS'ing up to 15 cells from the home anchor. The walk reveals 3. Shipping
   fog without this left every starter crate under the 80% wash on a fresh
   save, with the quest pointing at a road nobody could see. HOME_REVEAL_CELLS
   (10) covers the tutorial pocket the seater clears — the player's own block,
   which they are not discovering — and TRAIL_REVEAL_CELLS (5) rings each crate
   and the relic chest, found by the same `chest_start_` id stamp the gold
   onboarding arrow keys off. Deliberately NOT one blanket radius around the
   anchor: that would reveal map in every direction, including the way the
   trail does not go. starter_relic.test.js drives the real fog lift against
   the real seater, so moving either can't re-hide the trail.
 - Fog DEFERS to the UNMAPPED VEIL. A cell whose map tile hasn't arrived is
   already drawn as the animated survey-line fog that means "loading"; stacking
   the 80% wash on that would smother the one thing it exists to show. A fully
   veiled cell is left alone and becomes ordinary fog the moment its tile
   lands. That handover happens mid-fade on no cell crossing of its own, so
   while anything on screen is still veiled the fog pass stays dirty and
   rebuilds every frame — a few frames at the loading frontier, which is
   exactly the window where the image is genuinely changing.
 - tools/layer_audit.js pins the layer above every world layer AND checks the
   pass paints scene.fogTex rather than the terrain graphics — the same bug that
   left biome seams glowing outside the reach bubble, one layer up.

TEXTURES
 - EVERY opened chest VANISHES, crates included — there is no "looted" sprite.
   An opened low-tier / starter crate used to stay on the map as an open-lid
   marker: first with an 'open_box' texture that nothing registered (Phaser
   resolved the key to its __MISSING checkerboard), then with a procedural
   stand-in. The empty crate read as broken art either way, and an emptied crate
   is worth less on the map than the clear cell under it, so render.js now
   filters opened chests of every tier out of the render list identically. The
   tap target is unaffected (interactables.js still flashes "Picked clean
   already."), as are the POI pad and label, which persist via objList.
 - A texture key nothing creates fails SILENTLY — Phaser substitutes __MISSING
   rather than throwing. tools/shell_audit.js therefore checks both directions:
   every literal key RENDER_SPEC names is provided (by ASSETS, by a textures.js
   canvas, or by an app.js preload), and every make*Texture in textures.js is
   REACHABLE at boot (directly from app.js or via another maker — several are
   internal helpers). Keys computed from data (a house's role, a tree's
   species, a ground stack's item icon) are skipped rather than guessed at.

CHROME — salvaged field kit
 - One --chrome-* token vocabulary in index.html paints every persistent HUD
   surface, so they read as parts of one machine. The machine is SALVAGE:
   warm olive-brown weathered steel, dusty (not white) lit edges, and a faint
   brushed scuff layer. Panels are dark metal with a lit top lip and a cast

VERTICAL BUDGET (fitGame -> layOutVertically, index.html)
 - Three bands share the screen height and only the middle one is the game:
   TOP_CHROME (106px: money/energy row + objective chip), the map, and
   INV_CLUSTER (148px: category tabs + item bar + name + action buttons).
 - The game box used to be pinned to top:0 with the HUD pinned to the viewport
   bottom, so ALL the slack landed in one unmeasured place. That read very
   differently per device: an iPhone SE had the walking stick sitting 84px ON
   TOP of the map while a tall desktop window had a 195px hole under it.
 - Now the scale is capped by the phone-column width, and the slack is spent in
   priority order: the walking stick's room first, then a breathing margin
   (<=28px) under the objective chip.
 - ON A PHONE THE WIDTH WINS. A portrait viewport narrower than the column cap
   always fills edge to edge; only a viewport WIDER than a phone column (tablet
   portrait, desktop) gets gutters. Capping the scale by the leftover band as
   well is the bug that put black bars down both sides of an ordinary iOS
   Safari session and shrank the whole HUD with them: the band drops below the
   width fit as soon as vh - 254 < vw, which is what a 393x630 viewport (a
   393-wide phone with the tab bar showing) is. Nothing in the code has to
   change for it to happen — only the browser's toolbars.
 - What gives instead is the VERTICAL budget, in this order: the map slides up
   under the objective chip (never past TOP_ROW, 44px — the money/energy row is
   never covered, the chip retires itself), then at most one ring of cells
   tucks under the inventory. A viewport too short for even that shrinks the
   column after all, so the degenerate case degrades instead of burying the
   map. Desktop emulation keeps the old height cap: it never promised to fill
   a window it is only simulating a phone inside.
 - The stick is placed by fitGame into --stick-bottom: centred in the gap
   between the map and the tabs when it fits there, otherwise tucked just above
   the tabs, overlaying the map's bottom corner. Occluding a corner of the
   world beats occluding the inventory, and the stick never reaches the map's
   centre, where the player is drawn.
 - tools/layout_audit.js lifts layOutVertically out of the page and pins these
   invariants across eight real device sizes AND the same phones as a browser
   sees them (innerHeight with the toolbars on screen — 80-120px shorter, and
   the case the gutter bug lived in).

================================================================================
ARCHITECTURE: STANDARDIZED MODULES
================================================================================
The source has been progressively factored out of the monolithic app.js so the
common, repeated logic lives in one declarative or pure place. This is design
intent, not just cleanup — adding new content should mean editing data/cores,
not threading another branch through the dispatcher.

 - INTERACTABLE REGISTRY (interactables.js): every tap-driven WORLD OBJECT
   (tree, mineral rock, fruit tree, ground stack, chest, well, house, tower,
   shrine) is one declarative entry in a single INTERACTABLES table instead of a
   long if/else chain on o.kind. Each entry separates the three concerns that
   used to be tangled together:
     * tool          — relic slot that drives the work-wheel duration/speed
     * spent + spentAction — is it already consumed? ('skip' falls through so a
                       chopped stump doesn't block tilling; 'consume' eats the tap)
     * gate(o,save)  — pure check returning a flash string to BLOCK the tap
                       (e.g. "Need a Wood pick…"), or null to allow
     * energy(save,o)— up-front energy cost (also the cancel refund)
     * complete / custom — the payout / side effects when work finishes (custom
                       is the escape hatch for non-tool flows: instant fruit
                       harvest, chest ceremony, well refill, shop/shrine UIs)
   A single runInteractable(ctx, o) driver walks gate → spend → work-wheel, or
   defers to custom. Adding a new interactable = adding a row.
 - LUCK applies to CHEST/treasure loot only, read off `save` by pickReward() in
   rarity.js: TIER luck (the ring → rarer pulls, ringLuck) and QUANTITY luck
   (save.qtyUpgrades → bigger stacks, qtyLuck). Both are wizard-tower rungs;
   quantity was the amulet's until Sep 2026. Gather drops (wood,
   ore, gems, fruit) are not luck-aware: the registry carried a declarative
   `luck` field and a GATHER_LUCK_DEFAULT flag for it that shipped OFF and was
   never turned on, and both were removed rather than left as dead weight.
 - PURE CORES extracted from app.js for headless testing (no scene, no DOM); the
   scene keeps thin wrappers that own only the side effects (persistSave, DOM
   rebuilds, flashes):
     * inventory.js — stack/cap/dedupe rules over save.inv (one stack per id;
       cap = stackCapForBags, 9 → 249 at T7; excess rejected — no ground drops)
     * energy.js    — max/spend/offline-rest/tired-threshold math; single home
       of OFFLINE_FULL_REST_MS
     * crops.js     — growth / watering / pest rules; the single source of truth
       for STAGE_HOLD_MS (15 min/stage), which used to be copy-pasted across
       app.js / render.js / interact.js
     * home.js      — HomeArea hub: the canonical "am I near spawn?" test and the
       home-area tuning that isn't bound to one specific shop (e.g. the softwood
       home grove). Loaded before worldgen so generation can ask it mid-build.
