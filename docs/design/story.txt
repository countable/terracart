DRAGON HOOD — THE STORY (basis document)
================================================================================
Status: v6 (Oct 2026), the dragon archaeologist relationship added.
This file is the story bible the game is written against. Where it disagrees with copy in src/
(memory_story.js, play_tips.js, books, item text), THIS FILE wins and the copy
is to be brought in line. Numbers (which memory, which restoration, which cave
level) stay owned by the code; this file names the beats, not the values,
except where a beat IS a number the story needs (see ACT STRUCTURE).

Anything still marked [OPEN] is not yet canon; the short list is at the end.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. THE PREMISE IN ONE BREATH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You are the Hood: a quiet survivor who never takes the hood down, mending a
broken world one roof at a time. You are a dragon in a bipedal body, and you
do not know it. Nobody does. Fifty years ago your sister's spell took your
dragon body and your memories, caught in the heat of a battle between your
father, a five-headed hydra who conquers worlds through his children, and the
two children who would not burn this one. That battle broke most of the
planet. Your father walks it now as a kindly old wizard called Tim, raising
dragon hunters among the survivors, because he needs a living dragon of each
of his five colours to get his power back, and the two he needs most are his
own children. Your sister hides dragons deep under the earth so he never can,
and climbs to the surface to kill his hunters, which is why every survivor you
meet will tell you dragons are vicious things that ended the world.

The game is the mending. The story is what you remember while you mend.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE WORLD: THE SIXTH PLANET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The sixth planet: the sixth world Tiamat set out to take, the first he did
  not. Survivors have no name for it beyond "the world"; a scholar's book may
  call it "the sixth" without knowing what it is sixth of. [OPEN: a name.]
- What the player walks on is the real map: a neighbourhood going back to
  meadow, its houses wrecks, its roads intact, its street lamps dark. The
  survivors call that night THE BREAKING ("the night the roofs fell"). Fifty
  years on, trees stand in the lanes; the survivors are old, or the children
  of the ones who were. The place the player starts in is MENDING LANE; the
  name survives on a bent street sign and in the mouths of the neighbours.
- CAVES run beneath the whole surface, level after level. The first levels
  are danger; the deep ones are legend. Ayo's hidden dragons sit at the
  deepest levels a player can reach, and the hunts for them are the game's
  very late endgame (see ACT 3).
- SHRINES, GROVES, THE OLD STONES, THE SHORE: the sacred places predate the
  Breaking and were spared by it. Their keepers (the Nexus keepers, npc.js)
  are the only people who never left. The boons the shrines lend are the
  world's own small leftover magic, not a dragon's.
- THE ROADS AND LAMPS: the one thing the Breaking did not touch. The
  survivors' superstition holds that a road is safe ground (the game's kerb
  rule is that superstition made true).

2. THE FAMILY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TIAMAT — "TIM"
  A hydra: five heads, five colours (red, white, and three more), a conqueror
  of worlds. He takes a world by sending his children ahead as generals; he
  had two, a red and a white, and each is one of his heads made flesh. His
  POWER is the five colours bound to him; without a living dragon of each,
  the heads are only shapes. Fifty years ago that power was stripped (not his
  memories) when he clashed with his children over this world's fate. He
  REMEMBERS EVERYTHING. He is the only one who does.
  He lives now in human form as a wise old wizard who goes by Tim: warm,
  patient, generous with lessons. He KNOWS the Hood is his child from the
  first look. He says nothing. He needs the five colours brought to him ALIVE
  and bound; his own two children are the two he cannot catch himself, so he
  raises DRAGON HUNTERS from the survivors and, above all, raises the Hood,
  who will one day carry the white dragon to him and be the red.
  Tim never lies in his own voice. Everything he says is true and incomplete.
  He needs the Hood STRONGER, so he encourages every battle: the wild
  factions in the caves and on the surface are mostly incidental creeps, but
  some of them are secretly HIS, sent against the Hood as sparring partners
  and against the survivors as reasons to come to his tower.

THE FROST DRAGON QUEEN — TIAMAT'S LATE WIFE
  Tiamat was married to the Frost Dragon Queen and fought a war against her.
  She is dead. This is part of the wider history of dragons across the
  planets, not another name for Ayo or a replacement explanation for the
  Breaking. The cause of the war, the circumstances of her death, and whether
  she was the mother of the Hood and Ayo remain [OPEN]; do not invent those
  answers in a book or conversation. Her personal name is also [OPEN].
  From Act 2, the archaeologist can discuss the historical Tiamat and the
  Queen through fragmentary accounts, without knowing that the local wizard
  is Tiamat. Their history is not part of his Act 1 introduction.

AYO — THE SISTER
  A WHITE dragon, the strongest will of the children. It was her spell, fifty
  years ago, that stripped Tiamat of his power, and in the heat of that battle
  it caught her brother too: the Hood's body and memory went with it, BY
  ACCIDENT, the Hood between her and the father when the spell landed. She has
  carried that for fifty years. She hides dragons of the other colours deep
  beneath the earth so Tiamat can never assemble five, and she comes up to the
  surface, rarely, to kill his dragon hunters and to look for him. She does
  not hide what she is when she comes. That is where the lore comes from: a
  white dragon seen over a burning hunters' camp, every decade or so, is why
  survivors say dragons are vicious things that ended the world.
  Ayo is an ENEMY the player can fight and an ALLY the player can reach: she
  is the terror of the deep until the player learns what she is protecting,
  and she is the whole of two of the three endings.
  [OPEN: does Ayo know the Hood survived, and where? Draft: she knows the
  spell spared the Hood's life but not where the body went; she has looked
  for fifty years, under the earth, in the wrong direction.]

THE HOOD — THE PLAYER
  A RED dragon. Six hundred years old of a fifteen-hundred-year span; fifty
  years is a season, and the human body has not aged a day, which is the
  first crack an old neighbour can notice (an elder who swears you have not
  changed since the first years after the Breaking). Once Tiamat's general on
  five worlds; the survivors of the fifth called them the WARMONGER and the
  name followed them here. Lost the full dragon body and all memory in Ayo's
  spell. Kept the MAGIC: the player's recovered memories ARE that magic
  coming back (the memory ledger), the shrines answer it, and the fire
  breath, when it returns, was always theirs.
  Wears the hood always. It hides what a human body cannot: unnaturally
  bright red hair, sharp teeth, a forked tongue. The Hood keeps the hood up
  by a habit older than their memory and never explains it; survivors assume
  a burn, a vow, grief. Nobody asks twice. The Hood does not know what is
  under it. The game never says it in the player's hearing until memory 30.

THERE ARE NO OTHER SIBLINGS. The dragons Ayo hides are dragons of this world
and of the five before, the other three colours, gathered and kept below.
This world had dragons of its own: smaller, animal, and the lore's "vicious
creatures" has a grain of them in it too. The red dragon of the deep caves
(level 9 and below) is one of these wild ones, not family.

3. FIFTY YEARS AGO: THE BREAKING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What happened, in order (the truth; the player learns it out of order, see
ACT STRUCTURE):
1. Tiamat brought the family to the sixth planet as he had to five before:
   children first, the Warmonger (the Hood) at their head, fire over the first
   towns. The survivors' "night the roofs fell" began as the Hood's fire.
2. The children would not finish it. [OPEN: why this world and not the five
   before. Draft: Ayo had walked it as a human first, as she always scouted,
   and came back changed; she brought the Hood to see what she had seen. The
   Hood turned last, and turned hardest.]
3. Tiamat came down himself to end the argument. The clash of the hydra and
   his children over the capital is what broke the planet: not a war of
   armies, one family in one sky, and the ground under them. The roofs that
   the Hood's fire had not taken, the battle took; the Hood's fire was IN
   PART the cause, in the conflict with Tiamat, and the player's memories will
   not let them sort their share from his.
4. Ayo cast the spell. Its aim was Tiamat's POWER. It took that, and, with the
   Hood between them in the heat of it, it took the Hood's body and memory
   too. An accident. The one thing Ayo has never said aloud.
5. The backlash emptied the sky. Tiamat fell as a man. The Hood fell as a
   hooded stranger with no name. Ayo went below. Most of the world's people
   were already gone.

4. NOW: THE THREE WHO WALK THE SURFACE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE HOOD mends. Peacefully, roof by roof, field by field. They have no idea
why the old roads feel familiar, why the wrecks look like something they
should apologise for, or why the hood stays up.

TIM recruits. His tower is a wreck like the rest until the player raises it
(the Wizard Tower is on offer from the thirtieth restoration). Once it
stands, he is simply THERE, as if he had always been, warm and interested, and the memories the player brings him buy
power (the tower's rungs). He teaches the player to hunt. Every lesson is true
and every lesson serves him: a stronger Hood brings him his colours faster.
His DRAGON HUNTERS are the survivors' bravest, armed at his tower, and they go
into the caves after "the dragons that did this" and mostly do not come back,
because of Ayo. He tells the survivors Ayo did it. He is right.

AYO hides and hunts. Her dragons are deep. Her visits to the surface are rare
and terrible and dominate the survivors' dragon stories. Orrin offers a
competing account from older evidence. The night's ghosts and the
cave's demons are NOT hers: they are the world's own creeps, incidental, and
some of them are Tim's. She is met in person only at the end.

THE SURVIVORS believe: the Warmonger burned the world (true), dragons are
vicious (half true), the wise wizard is their hope (false), and the hooded
stranger is one of them (false). This is the prevailing belief, not unanimous:
Orrin, the dragon archaeologist, openly disagrees and is dismissed as a
crackpot. Most neighbours and local accounts reflect the prevailing belief;
his evidence and the older writings he studies make room for dissent.
See WHO MAY SAY WHAT.

4a. ORRIN — THE DRAGON ARCHAEOLOGIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PLACE AND FIRST IMPRESSION
  Orrin is present from the beginning, at a modest dig and cluttered field
  shelter near the edge of the starting Home area, about 250 metres from
  Home. This is his starting placement, not a change to the Home ward's
  mechanical radius. Put him beside a reachable path or verge, not behind
  inaccessible ground. He is available before any memory or restoration gate.
  "Orrin" is his working name. He introduces himself as a dragon archaeologist,
  catches his satchel on his own stool, and checks the potsherds before his
  bruised knee. He mislays his pencil behind his ear, labels the wrong bag,
  and apologises to objects he knocks over. His fieldwork is painstaking even
  when the rest of him is disorganised. The joke is his clumsiness, not his
  care for creatures or his willingness to question the village.

WHAT HE THINKS — AUTHOR KNOWLEDGE, NOT AN ACT 1 EXPOSITION DUMP
  He believes dragons across the planets are peaceful creatures, not violent
  by nature. Their size and fire do not tell him what they want. In old
  fragments he finds signs of nesting, tending young, long journeys and lives
  lived beside other creatures; he looks for ordinary life where others look
  only for weapons. These are his interpretations of incomplete evidence,
  not an omniscient account of every dragon species.
  He suspects that dragons attack people chiefly because people hunt them,
  steal from their homes and pursue their young. He does not believe dragons
  destroyed the world. Most people call him a crackpot, sometimes to his face.
  His challenge to inherited fear is valuable; his blanket denial of dragon
  involvement in the Breaking is wrong. The Hood and Tiamat did terrible harm.
  He must eventually distinguish a nature that permits peace from particular
  dragons' choices to make war. Understanding an attack does not erase its
  victims, and a dragon can still choose cruelty.
  He speaks of planets through old star charts, inscriptions and conflicting
  accounts. He has not personally visited them or witnessed their history.
  He knows the name Tiamat from those accounts, including the war against the
  Frost Dragon Queen, Tiamat's late wife. He does not recognise Tim as Tiamat,
  identify the Hood, name Ayo early, or turn a queen's title into proof of the
  white dragon's identity. He admits when an inscription does not tell him
  enough; other times he wants his conclusion to be true a little too much.

WHEN HE OPENS UP
  ACT 1 is acquaintance, not a history lesson. He mostly seems bumbling,
  eccentric and rather improbable: struggling with his stool, sorting labels
  he has mixed up, losing the thread of his own introduction. A passing remark
  may suggest that dragons are not naturally cruel, but he gives no sustained
  argument or persuasive display of evidence. The neighbours' "crackpot"
  judgement seems understandable. His humanity is visible before his insight.
  Do not name Tiamat or the Frost Dragon Queen in his Act 1 dialogue, explain
  the war, tour the planets, or recite his theory of the Breaking. Extra taps,
  books brought to him or unusually fast collecting cannot bypass this gate.
  ACT 2 begins opening his research, after the first tower introduction has
  actually happened and the Hood has met him. Now he can follow an odd remark
  with the evidence behind it: peaceful dragon lives, hunting provoking
  attacks, other planets, and finally the disputed accounts of Tiamat and
  the Frost Dragon Queen. Spread these across visits and relevant questions;
  do not unload them all at the act transition. He remains an eccentric,
  fallible friend rather than becoming a different, suddenly fluent person.

THE RELATIONSHIP
  The Hood first gives him something rarer than agreement: time to finish a
  sentence. Orrin remembers being listened to. On later visits he leaves a
  clear place to sit, shows a drawing he has saved for them, and asks what
  they actually saw before offering an explanation. Small warmth accumulates
  through remembered encounters, not through the same introduction repeating.
  The player may doubt him, bring observations that contradict him, or simply
  listen. Friendship is not a reward for choosing his opinion. He should ask
  after the Hood's tired hands or dust-caught hood as well as their discoveries;
  the player becomes a person he cares about, not a convenient collector.
  Other survivors tolerate him, mock his theories, or worry that he will get
  someone killed. An attack survivor can reasonably be angry with him. He is
  hurt by the ridicule and sometimes laughs along too quickly, but does not
  answer someone's loss with a joke. The Hood need not humiliate a neighbour
  to stand beside him, and no scene makes every sceptic foolish or malicious.

CANON RELATIONSHIP BEATS — ORDERED, ON THE EXISTING TRACKS
  1. From the beginning: Orrin is at the Home-edge dig. At the first meeting
     he introduces himself, fumbles with his things, and lets slip that the
     neighbours think him a crackpot. At most he offers a small, unsupported
     remark that dragons might not be so bad. Later relationship beats
     require this introduction; no restoration is required to meet him.
  2. Early mending: after two wreck restorations, he begins treating the Hood as a familiar
     visitor. He notices what they have repaired and has made room for them
     among his papers. His welcome changes because they have met before,
     not because the player has agreed with his theory.
  3. Act 2, the first tower and hunters: after the first tower introduction,
     he begins sharing the research behind his odd remarks, a piece at a time.
     Once the hunters are established in canon, he worries about people
     setting out to hunt dragons. He asks what a dragon
     was doing before someone raised a weapon, without yet understanding
     whose purposes the hunts serve. No early accusation that Tim is Tiamat.
  4. After the witnessed tower attack: he cannot dismiss what the Hood saw.
     He asks whether the dragon struck the tower or pursued fleeing people,
     but accepts the player's actual evidence rather than supplying an
     answer. His first concern is who was hurt; his theory comes second.
  5. After the identity revelation and the Act 3 dragon encounters: he can
     respond to the Hood's account of understanding a dragon. If he witnesses
     dragon speech himself, his excitement becomes a question about what the
     dragon said, followed by concern about whether anyone listened. Speech
     alone does not tell him the Hood's identity or family.
  6. As the truth of the Breaking becomes shareable: he revises his denial
     rather than declaring that he was right all along. Dragons did destroy
     lives; that does not make every dragon a born destroyer. His care for
     the Hood rests on their choices now, not on proving his theory correct.
     Do not grant him private revelations automatically or make his approval
     a gate on the player's ending.
  Memory supplies knowledge; restoration supplies the returning people and
  places he reacts to. Later responses require their witnessed events, not
  merely a high counter. His arc can wait if the player visits less often.
  These beats add no new numeric thresholds or compulsory collection grind.

LORE — OPTIONAL VISITS AND FINDINGS
  Between the required relationship beats, he can discuss a rubbing, a broken
  carving, a disputed translation or the dragon history in a book. These
  conversations can be encountered independently within their act's limits.
  In Act 1 they are small observations and eccentric diversions; substantive
  dragon history waits until Act 2. Books and finds enrich the friendship but
  never carry the only explanation of a required relationship change.
  Topics include peaceful dragon lives on other planets, the difference
  between a hunting account and an observation, and Tiamat's war against the
  Frost Dragon Queen. He can correct an earlier translation and remember that
  the Hood was the one who questioned it. He cannot settle the family's open
  questions just because a new fragment has been found.

VOICE SAMPLES — NPC DIALOGUE, NOT THE PANEL NARRATOR
  Act 1 introduction: "Orrin. Dragon archaeologist. Mind the little stool; it has
  already defeated me twice, and I am trying not to take it personally."
  Act 1 odd remark: "I do not think dragons wake up wanting to be horrible.
  Mind you, I have never been there when one woke up."
  Act 2 hunting: "Most accounts begin with someone going out to kill a dragon.
  I keep wondering what the dragon was doing before that."
  Act 2, across the planets: "Different skies, different bones, the same little
  hollows for eggs. They seem to have spent a great deal of time making homes."
  Act 1 reputation: "They call me a crackpot. One of the pots really is cracked,
  but I do not think that is what they mean."
  Act 2, the Queen: "Tiamat went to war against the Frost Dragon Queen, his own wife.
  These pieces tell me that much; they do not tell me how to make sense of it."
  Friendship: "I cleared you a seat. The papers are on it again, but it was
  clear when I thought of you."
  After violence: "Tell me who was hurt first. The theory can wait."
  Revision: "I wanted them to be innocent because everyone wanted them dead.
  That was not quite the same as looking at what happened."

IMPLEMENTATION STATUS
  Orrin is implemented as a named NPC at a saved, reachable seat about 250
  metres from the starting Home. Existing saves gain him when that ground is
  loaded. He uses PixelSerial’s Old Man art for his map sprite and portrait, with
  the shared NPC movement and wound/rest rules.
  Acknowledged conversations persist his introduction, topics, replies and
  familiarity. Leaving a conversation does not consume it, and disagreement
  does not punish the relationship. Restoration and new memories inform his
  returning greetings. Research requires the acknowledged tower introduction
  (or an already completed Act 3 reveal on an older save), never a counter alone.
  Later reactions to the tower attack and witnessed dragon speech still await
  those world events; the conversation does not pretend they have happened.
  The Frost Dragon Queen history is newly established story canon, with its
  unresolved details kept open above. Orrin's peaceful-dragon interpretation
  and denial of the Breaking are his beliefs, not replacements for the truth
  of the family history.

5. ACT STRUCTURE (mapped onto the existing mechanics)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CANON AND LORE — TWO DELIVERY LAYERS
  CANON is the required, ordered story: the opening, plot-bearing recovered
  memories, NPC arrivals and changes, Tim's lessons, the tower attack and
  relocation, the identity reveal, dragon speech, and the later choices.
  These events establish what has happened and what the player can know.
  A required event may be a conversation, a world change, an NPC appearance
  or a story panel; the panel format itself does not make something canon.

  LORE is optional and asynchronous: books, ambient place panels, first-tool
  moments, the bare-handed-work vignette, ordinary discovery impressions,
  repeated conversations, and other glimpses of life in the world. The
  player may miss these or encounter them in different orders. They deepen
  the story but never carry the only explanation needed for a canon event.
  Lore still obeys the story bible: optional does not mean untrue. Books
  keep their authors' voices; narrated panels keep the Hood's sensory voice.
  Both layers respect the same revelation gates, including no identity
  reveal before the second-tower revelation at 30 memories.

TWO ROUGHLY PARALLEL TIMELINES
  MEMORY advances what the Hood remembers and understands. RESTORATION
  advances the returning community, its homes, and the places where events
  can happen. They are separate lifetime counters, not interchangeable
  points or a fixed conversion. Either track may lead the other.
  The first find of each discoverable thing returns a memory; milestone
  scenes currently land on every third memory. Restored houses determine
  building roles and available homes. Existing thresholds remain owned by
  src/memory_story.js, src/houses.js and src/npc.js.

  Milestone                   Memory track          Restoration / world track

  Towers are raised by the player's choice at a wreck (houses.js
  BUILD_OPTIONS): the Wizard Tower card is offered from the thirtieth
  restoration until the first stands, and again from the fifty-second (with
  21 memories) until the second does. References below to "restoration 30"
  or "52+" are when the card becomes available, not a fixed restoration.
  The milestones are eligibility conditions, not a promise that both tracks
  reach the same point together. For example, recovering 30 memories before
  restoring the first tower must not skip meeting Tim or his required lessons.

CANON ORDERING CONTRACT
  A canon event needs its milestone conditions, its world context, and its
  earlier required events. Reaching a threshold makes it eligible; it does
  not mean the event has occurred or its revelation is known. Keep it pending
  until it can happen in sequence, and record completion only when the event
  actually occurs (and its required presentation is acknowledged).
  Count thresholds use "at least", so progressing past a milestone cannot
  lose it. Pending canon does not freeze ordinary discovery or restoration.
  An NPC's required arrival or change belongs to canon; tapping every optional
  line that NPC can say does not. A required conversation is identified as an
  event of its own. Repeated observations after it remain lore.
  Lore never blocks canon. It may wait for an appropriate place, action or
  knowledge state, and should not interrupt a canon sequence in progress.

IMPLEMENTATION STATUS
  This is the intended delivery structure, not a claim that all ordering is
  implemented. Today the first tower becomes abandoned at 21 memories even
  if earlier conversations were missed, and the second-tower reveal can
  precede unread lessons. Those shortcuts need reconciliation with required
  canon ordering. The witnessed attack, vanished tower and dragon-language
  reactions remain specified story events awaiting gameplay implementation.
  The split changes neither existing numeric gates nor save keys by itself.

ACT 1 — THE MENDING (memories 0–8, before the tower)
  The Hood wakes at the trailer with nothing. Tilly, a child, is the only
  neighbour there (homeless until the first restoration after they meet).
  Neighbours return as memories do. The story neighbours arrive by memory
  count: Bryn the warden at three explains the safe area on first meeting
  (nobody knows why only weak monsters live here; the truth: the Hood's own
  leftover magic, which Tim will later learn to read); Maud the survivor at
  six, met hunted by a goblin archer, who stays where she is saved and tells
  of the night the roofs fell and names the Warmonger; Edda the believer at
  nine (lauds the wizard: "restore his tower and he will save us").
  Memory 3: a bell in the smoke, a child held, the name Warmonger. Memory 6: a
  still figure watching from a window (Tim, who was there). Memory 9: the
  rumour of a wise man "who may know why the old roads feel familiar".
  The player learns: the world was burned; a dragon did it; a wizard might
  help. Nothing says what the player is.
  THE WORK OF MANY HANDS: the first time the Hood finishes substantial
  labour without a tool, survivors stop to watch. In the tree-felling scene,
  the Hood braces against the trunk and tears through it with bare hands.
  The tree gives way before the neighbours have finished fetching an axe.
  "That would have taken us all morning," one says. Another looks at the
  Hood's empty hands. "Where is your tool?" The Hood has no answer.
  This is astonishing speed and strength beside ordinary human labour;
  tools still make the Hood's work quicker. The hood stays raised. Bark,
  movement and shadow keep the hands ambiguous: a suggestion of claws may
  be noticed, but no unmistakable dragon anatomy is shown before memory 30.
  The vignette follows successful work and depicts what the player actually
  did. A tree falls only if that job felled a tree; other bare-handed work
  needs a matching scene. It is witnessed once, without turning every job
  into another revelation. Survivors know the Hood is unusually strong;
  they do not learn what the Hood is.
  TOOL STORIES: the existing axe illustration belongs to the first actual
  use of an owned axe. Bare-handed work has its own story and must not mark
  the axe story as seen. The same rule applies to other depicted tools: the
  pictured tool must be the one used for the action.

ACT 2 — THE LESSONS (the first tower, memories 9–21)
  Tim's tower is raised by the player, on offer from the thirtieth restoration. It is sealed until nine memories.
  Inside, Tim is kind, asks for memories, gives power. His lessons (the
  existing beats: "In the right order", "What remains useful", "An old
  habit", "Their little lives", "What comes next") are the hydra teaching the
  general: remember the victory, not the doorway; move when told; the small
  lives are not yours to carry. He is REBUILDING THE WARMONGER for his own
  use. The player's recovered memories contradict him one by one (the Act 2
  memory panels): the raised hand was braced against a door, not welcoming;
  the "good habit" was the grip of a handler; the mark on the map was a town.
  THE HUNTERS: dragon hunters are NPCs. They gather at the tower once it
  stands, go down into the caves in Tim's name, and are missed; a notice of
  the missing, a widow among the neighbours, a hunter met in a cave who has
  seen "the white one". They are the survivors' bravest and Tim's spent coin.
  THE SPARRING: Tim sends the Hood against the wild factions, and some of what
  the Hood fights is his (a garrison that should not be there, a foe that
  turns up when he has just said it would). Never stated; a reader who
  reads the books in order can work it out.
  THE WHITE ONE AT THE TOWER: midway through the wider Act 2 arc, at the
  existing first-tower departure gate (21 memories), Ayo attacks Tim's tower
  in full white-dragon form. The player witnesses the attack. Her shadow
  crosses the lamps; she strikes the tower, and the whole tower vanishes.
  The road and lamps remain. This replaces the quiet abandoned-tower visit:
  there is no empty room to enter, only the place where the tower stood.
  Tim survives and is next found at the second tower. The exact magic of the
  disappearance remains unexplained here; the attack causes it, rather than
  an unrelated move happening off screen. His invitation, "come to me when
  you are ready," reaches the Hood afterward, outside the vanished tower.
  The witnesses call the attacker "the white one". To them, the attack
  proves that the dragon hunters need their wizard more than ever. Nobody
  names Ayo or reveals the family; the Hood does not yet speak dragon.

ACT 2b — THE SECOND TOWER (restoration 52+, memories 21–30)
  The Hood restores the second tower among the wrecks after the first has
  vanished. Its hunters speak of the white dragon's attack. Tim's warmth is
  thinner; the map is open on his table ("this land will seem very small"). At memory
  30 he tells the Hood what they are and what he needs, plainly: you are a
  dragon, and so am I; the fire above the ruined town was your own; the
  Warmonger was you. And the ask: CAPTURE THE WHITE DRAGON, alive, and bring
  her to me, and my power is restored. He does not say she is their sister.
  He does not say the Hood is the red. He has three colours waiting below and
  needs two.

ACT 3 — THE FIRE BELOW (after memory 30; the hunts are very late game)
  The player goes down for their fire (underground depth 9, the existing DragonStory
  reward) and keeps going. Below are Ayo's hidden dragons, one colour at a
  time, each bound in her keeping; the HUNTS are the capture of each, alive
  (the net and the catch wheel are the tools; a dragon that dies is a colour
  lost to everyone). And at the bottom, the white dragon.
  THE LANGUAGE COMES BACK: during Act 3, after the memory-30 revelation, a
  dragon speaks in the presence of the Hood and a surviving dragon hunter.
  The Hood understands and answers before thinking. The hunter lowers their
  weapon: "That was no spell. You spoke to it." The Hood is surprised too;
  the words feel older than any lesson Tim gave them. This is recovered
  dragon speech, not a skill Tim has just taught or a potion effect.
  Later, a second survivor hears the Hood answer a dragon and stops in
  astonishment: "It answered you. How did you know what to say?" People can
  learn that the Hood speaks dragon without learning that the Hood IS one.
  The exchange lets a dragon be heard as a person before the Hood chooses
  whether to capture or free it. Speaking grants understanding, not obedience
  or an automatic capture. The hood stays raised throughout.
  The player learns, from the hidden dragons and from Ayo herself, what Tim
  left out: that the white is their sister, that the spell was an accident,
  that the five colours bound to the hydra are the end of this world and the
  next.
  THREE ENDINGS:
  (a) THE GENERAL: deliver the colours and the white dragon to Tim. The hydra
      rises; the Hood's dragon body is given back by the father who took the
      family to five worlds; the sixth planet is his; the mending was for
      nothing. Playable, and the only ending with the dragon body restored.
  (b) THE SISTER: stand with Ayo. Free what the hunts bound, and face Tim; a
      man with all his memories and none of his power. The Hood keeps the
      hood and the fire and never gets the body back. The family stays
      broken so the world can stay mended.
  (c) THE PACIFIST: neither. Refuse the hunts, release every dragon the player
      caught, and go home to the lane. Tim is left waiting in a tower nobody
      visits; Ayo is left below with her charges; the Hood mends. Nothing is
      resolved and nothing is burned. The title's own ending.
  [OPEN: what becomes of Tim in (b): draft, not killed; left among the people
  he meant to burn, seen once more mending a roof.]

6. WHO MAY SAY WHAT (the spoiler gates)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hard rules, enforced by the tests (memory_arc, story_neighbours, books):
- Before memory 30, nothing in the player's hearing says the Hood is a dragon,
  that the wizard is their father, or that the wizard is choosing what to give
  back. Foreshadowing is allowed and encouraged: the familiar roads, the figure
  in the window, the elder who swears you have not aged, the hood nobody asks
  about, the safe area nobody can explain, the hair a child once glimpsed and
  was told was a trick of the light.
- "Dragons are vicious and ended the world" is the prevailing survivor belief,
  available to books, keepers, scouts, the believer and hunters at any time.
  Orrin is the exception: he seems like a bumbling crackpot in Act 1, with at
  most an odd, unsupported remark in a dragon's defence. In Act 2 he begins
  explaining his belief that dragons are peaceful by nature, hunted into
  violence, and not responsible for the Breaking. His dissent neither reveals
  the Hood nor makes his denial of the Breaking true.
- "The Warmonger" is the name survivors have for the one who burned the roofs.
  Anyone may use it. No one knows the Warmonger was a general's title and not
  a dragon's name; a scholar may wonder.
- Tim never lies in his own voice. Everything he says is true and incomplete.
  His tells are omissions: he never asks the Hood's name; he never looks at
  the hood; he never says "sister".
- Ayo is spoken of only as "the white one", "the thing under the hill", "what
  took the hunters". No one has her name before Act 3.
- The five colours are known lore ("five heads, five fires", a nursery rhyme
  [OPEN: write one, or let the books invent it]). Historical accounts may name
  Tiamat and his marriage and war with the Frost Dragon Queen. Orrin shares
  this history only from Act 2 onward and does not
  know that Tim is Tiamat or that the Hood and Ayo are his children; historical
  family knowledge must not identify the living family before its reveals.
- After memory 30 the believer still lauds the wizard; the survivor still
  curses the Warmonger; the child still has their room. The irony is the
  player's alone. Witnesses in Act 3 may discover and react to the Hood's
  dragon speech; that does not reveal the Hood's dragon identity or family.
  No neighbour learns those secrets unless an ending says so.

7. THINGS THE GAME ALREADY HAS THAT NOW MEAN SOMETHING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- THE HOOD on the player sprite: never comes down, in any pose, any potion,
  any skin. The Dragon Powder minute (the player draws as a dragon) is a
  glimpse of the truth the player reads as a potion effect.
- THE SAFE AREA (only weak monsters near Home): the Hood's own leftover magic.
  The warden's "for some reason" is answered at memory 30.
- THE WIZARD TOWER'S RUNGS (power for memories): Tim rebuilding his general.
- THE DRAGON HUNTERS: NPCs. The survivors' bravest, armed at the tower, lost
  in the caves; a notice board of the missing; a hunter's widow; a hunter
  deep down who has seen the white one.
- THE NIGHT'S GHOSTS, THE LAIRS, THE DEEP DEMONS: the world's own creeps,
  incidental; some of them Tim's, never announced.
- THE RED DRAGON at underground depth 9+: a wild dragon of this world, not family.
- THE NET AND THE CATCH WHEEL: the hunts' tools. Alive, or lost.
- SHRINE BOONS: the world's own small magic answering the Hood's.
- THE KERB RULE (the road is never a refuge for monsters, always for the
  player): the survivors' one true superstition, and a clue: the roads were
  spared because the general who burned the towns would not burn the roads
  they came by.
