# Road safety research

This file owns the research behind the game's road-safety mechanics, so every
safety rule in the code can name its evidence. The mechanics live where they
always do: [generation.md](generation.md) for zones and the spawn gate,
[combat.md](combat.md) for the energy drain, [rendering.md](rendering.md) for
the night ground cover, [presentation.md](presentation.md) for the safety
copy. The exact player-facing strings are mirrored in the code's safety
tables; if this file and the code disagree on copy, fix the code.

The stakes: this game is played while walking in the real world. A mechanic
that pushes a player toward traffic, or that rewards standing on a carriageway
with a screen up, is a bug even when the game logic is correct.

## 1. Night on arterial roads is where pedestrians die

What the data shows (United States, latest full-year reports):

| Finding | Figure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pedestrian fatalities in dark conditions | 78% (19% daylight, 2% dusk, 2% dawn) | NHTSA, Traffic Safety Facts: Pedestrians, 2022 data (DOT HS 813 590) |
| Peak fatal hours | 6-8:59pm (26%) and 9-11:59pm (25%) | same |
| Pedestrian deaths on arterials | 59% (38% principal + 21% minor arterial) | USDOT Pedestrian Safety Action Plan, Nov 2020 (FARS 2018); PBIC webinar 2021 |
| Arterials' share of road miles | 13% of non-interstate miles carry those 59% of deaths | PBIC/Toole Design, Pedestrian Safety on Arterial Streets, 2021 |
| Urban share | 85% of pedestrian fatalities | NHTSA 2022 |

So the overlap is real: most harm happens after dark, on wide, fast, urban
arterials - exactly our ROAD_MD / ROAD_LG group. Nighttime arterial crashes
also kill at vehicle speeds where survival is unlikely; the hot-spot study
below found over three quarters of top pedestrian fatality corridors posted
30 mph (48 km/h) or higher.

Game rules this feeds (owner decisions, logged here):

1. At night (the sun below the horizon, the same `SAFETY_DUSK_DAYLIGHT`
   threshold the dusk safety card reads), the Major-and-Medium road
   suppression zone (band + kerb buffer) becomes a no-play area:
   interactive objects there are hidden and cannot be tapped, the whole zone
   takes the red danger ground cover, and standing in it drains energy
   slowly. The player should read the street, not the screen, there.
2. The first time a player enters that zone at night, a story panel tells
   them to stop playing there:
   "Major roads at night are dangerous. It's best you ascend (turn off
   device) to avoid energy drain and accidents."
   "Ascend" is in-world for putting the phone away; the game's own metaphor
   for ending a session.

## 2. Wide crossings and junctions

| Finding | Figure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top US pedestrian fatality hot-spot corridors that are multilane | 97% | Schneider, Sanders & Proulx, TRB 2020 |
| Hot-spot corridors where crossing means 5+ lanes | 70% | same |
| Hot-spot corridors with speed limits 30 mph+ | over 75% | same |
| Pedestrian fatalities at non-intersection locations | 75% (16% at intersections) | NHTSA 2022 |

Crossing distance is the exposure: more lanes means more time in the road,
and junction area is where turning conflicts stack on top of through
traffic. NHTSA's own safety reminders tell pedestrians to cross at
crosswalks or intersections and to be predictable.

Game rules this feeds:

1. Any junction involving an MD/LG road carries a full exclusion zone: the
   overlapping road area plus a 2-cell buffer. Nothing spawns there and no
   enemy ever steps into it. (generation.md owns the exact stamp.)
2. Even small-road (SM) junctions carry a 1-cell suppression buffer: no
   fast movers and no hazards spawn or enter there. The buffer is typed,
   like the kerb, so slow flora-tier spawns and friendly fauna can still
   use the corner.

## 3. Research question: can we tell where sidewalks are?

Short answer: partially, and not from our current tile feed.

- OpenStreetMap records sidewalks two ways (OSM wiki, "Sidewalk"):
  1. Inline tags on the road way: `sidewalk=left|right|both|no|separate`.
     Cheap to map, common in Western Europe (53% of mapped sidewalk
     distance there), but carries no geometry of its own.
  2. Separate ways: `highway=footway` + `footway=sidewalk`. Spatially
     accurate and the dominant scheme worldwide as of April 2025, but
     harder to map and regionally uneven.
- Coverage is patchy and region-dependent everywhere; neither scheme
  reliably covers a given city, and absence of a tag does not mean absence
  of a sidewalk.
- Our MVT transportation layer carries `class` (and `brunnel` where the
  provider includes it), not the `sidewalk` attribute. Separate sidewalk
  ways DO arrive, but only as generic footway paths (`PATH_CLASSES` ->
  `T.PATH`), indistinguishable from any other footway. So today we can
  infer "a walkable way beside the road exists" but we cannot reliably
  distinguish sidewalk from path, and we cannot see inline sidewalks at
  all.
- Why it matters: research going back to Knoblauch et al. (1987), cited in
  FHWA's pedestrian synthesis, found sites without sidewalks were more
  than twice as likely to have pedestrian crashes. Where we cannot see a
  sidewalk, we must not assume one; the kerb buffer and the night zone are
  the conservative substitute. If a future tile source exposes
  `footway=sidewalk` subclassing, the kerb buffer could shrink onto the
  sidewalk itself - a separate decision, not taken here.

## 4. Standing on the roadway

NHTSA's pedestrian reminders name device distraction directly: stay alert,
do not be distracted by electronic devices, walk on a sidewalk or path when
one is available. Our GPS gameplay anchors the player to their real
position, so the game can detect the one state that must never be rewarded:
the player standing on the carriageway of a major road.

Game rule this feeds: when the player's own cell is majority-covered
(>50%) by an MD/LG road band, the game shows a dismissable safety warning -
"Do not play when standing on the road." - once per UTC day. The warning
fires on the road band itself, not the kerb, because pavement is where the
game expects play to happen; the carriageway is not.
