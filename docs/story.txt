DRAGON HOOD — THE STORY (basis document)
================================================================================
Status: DRAFT v1 (Oct 2026). This file is the story bible the game is written
against. Where it disagrees with copy in src/ (memory_story.js, play_tips.js,
books, item text), THIS FILE wins and the copy is to be brought in line.
Numbers (which memory, which restoration, which cave level) stay owned by the
code; this file names the beats, not the values, except where a beat IS a
number the story needs (see ACT STRUCTURE).

Anything marked [Q#] is an assumption awaiting the owner's answer; the
questions are listed at the end. Fill them in and delete the tags.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. THE PREMISE IN ONE BREATH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You are the Hood: a quiet survivor who never takes the hood down, mending a
broken world one roof at a time. You are a dragon in a bipedal body, and you
do not know it. Nobody does. Fifty years ago your sister's spell took your
dragon body and your memories to stop a war between your father, a hydra who
conquers worlds through his children, and the children who would not burn this
one. The spell broke most of the planet. Your father walks it now as a kindly
old wizard called Tim, gathering dragon hunters among the survivors, because
he needs a dragon of each of the five colours to get his power back. Your
sister hides dragons deep under the earth so he never can, and climbs to the
surface to kill his hunters — which is why every survivor you meet will tell
you dragons are vicious things that ended the world.

The game is the mending. The story is what you remember while you mend.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE WORLD: THE SIXTH PLANET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The sixth planet. The sixth world Tamiat set out to take; the first he did
  not. Survivors have no name for it beyond "the world"; a scholar's book
  may call it "the sixth" without knowing what it is sixth of. [Q1: the
  planet's name, if it has one, and whether the people know they are the
  sixth.]
- What the player walks on is the real map: a neighbourhood going back to
  meadow, its houses wrecks, its roads intact, its street lamps dark. The game
  calls this state THE BREAKING (the survivors' word; the night "the roofs
  fell"). Fifty years on, trees stand in the lanes and the survivors are old,
  or the children of the ones who were.
- CAVES run beneath the whole surface, level after level. The survivors know
  the first levels as dangerous and the deep ones as legend. Ayo's hidden
  dragons are below the deepest level the game lets a player reach. [Q2]
- SHRINES, GROVES, THE OLD STONES, THE SHORE: the sacred places predate the
  Breaking and were spared by it. Their keepers (zone keepers, npc.js) are the
  only people who never left. The boons the shrines lend are small leftover
  magic — the world's, not a dragon's.
- THE ROADS AND LAMPS: the one thing the Breaking did not touch was the
  roads. The survivors' superstition holds that a road is safe ground (the
  game's kerb rule is that superstition made true).

2. THE FAMILY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TAMIAT — "TIM"
  A hydra: five heads, five colours, a conqueror of worlds. He takes a world by
  sending his children ahead as generals; each child is a dragon of one colour
  and each colour is one of his heads. His power is not his own — it is drawn
  from his children, which is why losing them is losing it. [Q3: does each
  child's colour correspond to one of his five heads, and is his power literally
  the sum of his five children?]
  Fifty years ago his power was stripped (not his memories) when he clashed
  with his children over this world's fate. He REMEMBERS EVERYTHING. He is the
  only one who does. He lives now in human form as a wise old wizard who goes
  by Tim, warm, patient, generous with his lessons, and recruits DRAGON
  HUNTERS from the survivors. He needs one living dragon of each of the five
  colours brought to him to restore his power and his true form.
  [Q4: does he need the dragons alive, bound, or killed? "Assemble" reads as
  alive and bound; the hunters then CAPTURE, and the player's tools lean that
  way (the net, the catch wheel).]
  [Q5: does Tim KNOW the Hood is his child? Draft assumption: yes, from the
  first look — the hood hides the face, not the gait. He says nothing, and
  cultivates the Hood as his best hunter, so that his own child will carry his
  other children to him and, at the last, be the fifth.]

AYO — THE SISTER
  A dragon, one colour [Q6: which — draft assumes BLACK, the colour of the
  deep]. The strongest will of the children. It was HER spell, fifty years ago,
  that stripped the Hood of body and memory and Tamiat of his power, and that
  broke most of the planet. She hides dragons deep beneath the earth so Tamiat
  can never assemble five, and she comes up to the surface, rarely, to kill his
  dragon hunters and to look for him. She does not hide what she is when she
  comes. That is where the lore comes from: a black dragon seen over a burning
  hunters' camp, every decade or so, is why survivors say dragons are vicious
  things that ended the world.
  [Q7: does Ayo know the Hood survived, and where? Draft: she knows the spell
  spared the Hood's life but not where the body went; she has been looking for
  fifty years, under the earth, in the wrong direction.]
  [Q8: is Ayo an enemy the player can fight, an ally the player can reach, or
  both in order? Draft: both — she is the night's terror until the player
  learns what she is protecting, and the end-game ally after.]

THE HOOD — THE PLAYER
  A dragon [Q9: colour — draft assumes RED; the game's one dragon sprite, the
  fire breath recovered in Act 3, and "the fire above the ruined town"]. Once
  Tamiat's general on five worlds; the survivors of the fifth world called them
  the WARMONGER and the name followed them here. Lost the full dragon body and
  all memory in Ayo's spell, fifty years ago. Kept the MAGIC: the player's
  recovered memories ARE that magic coming back (the memory ledger), the
  shrines answer them, and the fire breath, when it returns, was always theirs.
  Wears the hood always. Never explained, never remarked on by the player;
  survivors assume a burn, a vow, grief. In truth the hood hides what a human
  body cannot quite hide: [Q10: WHAT does the hood hide — horns, scales at the
  hairline, eyes that catch the dark? Draft: the eyes.]
  The Hood does not know. Nobody does. The game never says it in the player's
  hearing until memory 30 (see ACT STRUCTURE).
  [Q11: the Hood's age and body — a human in their twenties? Fifty years have
  passed; does the human body age? Draft: it does not, and the survivors who
  remember a hooded stranger from the first years after the Breaking are the
  first crack in the story (an elder who swears you have not aged).]

THE OTHER THREE
  Tamiat has five children; the Hood and Ayo are two. The other three are the
  dragons Ayo hides beneath the earth. [Q12: are the hidden dragons the Hood's
  SIBLINGS, or other dragons of this and other worlds that Ayo gathered? Draft:
  the siblings — three colours, three generals, each hidden on a different
  deep level, each with a way of not wanting to be found.] [Q13: names and
  colours for the three.]

3. FIFTY YEARS AGO: THE BREAKING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What happened, in order (the truth; the player learns it out of order, see
ACT STRUCTURE):
1. Tamiat brought the family to the sixth planet as he had to five before:
   children first, the Warmonger (the Hood) at their head, fire over the first
   towns. The survivors' "night the roofs fell" is this. It was the Hood's
   fire. [Q14: confirm the Hood did the burning before the turn — the current
   game says so, and it is the hinge of the Act 2 reveal.]
2. The children would not finish it. [Q15: WHY this world and not the five
   before — draft: Ayo had walked it as a human first, as she always scouted,
   and this time came back changed; she brought the others to see what she
   had seen. The Hood turned last, and turned hardest.]
3. Tamiat came down himself to end the argument. The clash of the hydra and
   his children over the capital is what broke the planet: not a war of
   armies, one family in one sky, and the ground under them.
4. Ayo cast the spell. Its aim was Tamiat's POWER. It took that — and it took
   the Hood's body and memory with it. [Q16: on purpose or by accident? Draft:
   on purpose — the Hood was between them, the Warmonger shielding the father
   out of fifty years of habit, and Ayo chose to strip her brother rather than
   let the spell fail; she has never forgiven herself and never admitted it.
   This makes "removed them in a spell" literal: she removed the Hood from the
   fight.]
5. The spell's backlash emptied the sky. Tamiat fell as a man. The Hood fell as
   a hooded stranger with no name. Ayo and the three went below. Most of the
   world's people were already gone.

4. NOW: THE THREE WHO WALK THE SURFACE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE HOOD mends. Peacefully, roof by roof, field by field. They have no idea
why the old roads feel familiar, why the wrecks look like something they
should apologise for, or why the hood stays up.

TIM recruits. His tower is a wreck like the rest until the player restores it
(the fifteenth restoration). Once it stands, he is simply THERE, as if he had
always been, warm and interested, and the memories the player brings him buy
power (the tower's rungs). He teaches the player to hunt. Every lesson is true
and every lesson serves him: a stronger Hood brings him his children faster.
He never lies; he leaves things out. His dragon hunters are the survivors'
bravest, armed by him, and they go into the caves and do not come back —
because of Ayo. He tells the survivors Ayo did it. He is right.

AYO hides and hunts. Her dragons are deep. Her visits to the surface are rare
and terrible and are the whole of the dragon lore. [Q17: how do Ayo's visits
show in play? Draft: the night's ghosts and the deep-cave demons are HERS —
wards she set, not evil; the player meets her in person only at the end of
Act 2.]

THE SURVIVORS believe: the Warmonger burned the world (true), dragons are
vicious (half true), the wise wizard is their hope (false), and the hooded
stranger is one of them (false). Every neighbour's line and every book page
is written from inside that belief. See WHO MAY SAY WHAT.

5. ACT STRUCTURE (mapped onto the existing mechanics)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The engine of the story is the MEMORY LEDGER (src/memory_story.js): the first
find of each discoverable thing returns a memory. Scenes land on every third
memory. The gates below are the ones the code holds today; keep them.

ACT 1 — THE MENDING (memories 0–8, before the tower)
  The Hood wakes at the trailer with nothing. The warden explains the safe
  area (nobody knows why only weak monsters live here — the truth: the Hood's
  own leftover magic, which Tim will later learn to read). Neighbours return
  as memories do. The story neighbours by the trailer arrive by memory count:
  the believer (lauds the wizard, "restore his tower and he will save us"),
  the survivor (tells of the night the roofs fell, names the Warmonger), the
  child (homeless until the next restoration).
  Memory 3: a bell in the smoke, a child held, the name Warmonger. Memory 6: a
  still figure watching from a window (Tim, who was there). Memory 9: the
  rumour of a wise man "who may know why the old roads feel familiar".
  The player learns: the world was burned; a dragon did it; a wizard might
  help. Nothing says what the player is.

ACT 2 — THE LESSONS (the first tower, memories 9–21)
  The fifteenth restoration is Tim's tower. It is sealed until nine memories.
  Inside, Tim is kind, asks for memories, gives power. His lessons (the
  existing ACT2 beats: "In the right order", "What remains useful", "An old
  habit", "Their little lives", "What comes next") are the hydra teaching the
  general: remember the victory, not the doorway; move when told; the small
  lives are not yours to carry. He is REBUILDING THE WARMONGER, for his own
  use. The player's recovered memories contradict him one by one (the Act 2
  memory panels): the raised hand was braced against a door, not welcoming;
  the "good habit" was the grip of a handler; the mark on the map was a town.
  NEW IN DRAGON HOOD: the hunts. Tim sends the Hood down for "the dragon that
  did this" — in truth, for the siblings Ayo hid. [Q18: do the hunts exist as
  mechanics in this version (cave bosses to find and bind), or stay as story?]
  At 21 memories the first tower is cold and a note says "come to me when you
  are ready." He has moved — because Ayo found his tower. [Q19: confirm.]

ACT 2b — THE SECOND TOWER (restoration 25+, memories 21–30)
  The new tower among the wrecks the player restores. Tim's warmth is thinner;
  the map is open on his table ("this land will seem very small"). At memory
  30 the mask comes off: the old man's outline unfolds — [Q20: into WHAT? The
  current scene is a red dragon; in Dragon Hood he is a HYDRA with no power.
  Draft: the five-headed shadow of what he was, cast on the wall, and the one
  line: "I am a dragon, and so are you."] The fire above the ruined town was
  the Hood's own. The Warmonger was you.
  Tim's version of the rest: your fire is below, the demons have kept it;
  bring it back, bring your brothers and sister back, and we will leave this
  small world as it should have been left.

ACT 3 — THE FIRE BELOW (after memory 30)
  The player goes down for their fire (cave level 9, the existing DragonStory
  reward) and finds, past the demons, that the demons are AYO'S wards and
  that what Tim called "the dragon that did this" is a sibling in chains of
  Ayo's making, waiting fifty years for a father who must never come.
  The choice of the game: [Q21: IS there a choice? Draft: two endings —
  (a) deliver the siblings and yourself to Tim: the hydra rises, the sixth
  planet is taken, the mending was for nothing (the bad end, playable);
  (b) stand with Ayo: the Hood keeps the hood, keeps the fire, and the family
  stays broken so the world can stay mended. The Hood never gets the dragon
  body back in ending (b) — that is the price, and the title.]
  [Q22: what happens to Tim in ending (b)? Draft: he is not killed. A man with
  all his memories and none of his power is left among the people he meant to
  burn, to rebuild with them or not. He is seen once more, mending a roof.]

6. WHO MAY SAY WHAT (the spoiler gates)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hard rules, enforced by the tests (memory_arc, story_neighbours, books):
- Before memory 30, nothing in the player's hearing says the Hood is a dragon,
  that the wizard is their father, or that the wizard is choosing what to give
  back. Foreshadowing is allowed and encouraged: the familiar roads, the figure
  in the window, the elder who swears you have not aged, the hood nobody asks
  about, the safe area nobody can explain.
- "Dragons are vicious and ended the world" is what EVERY survivor believes
  and may say at any time. It is not a spoiler; it is the lore. Books, keepers,
  scouts, the believer: all of them.
- "The Warmonger" is the name survivors have for the one who burned the roofs.
  Anyone may use it. No one knows the Warmonger was a dragon general's title
  and not a dragon's name; a scholar may wonder.
- Tim never lies in his own voice. Everything he says is true and incomplete.
  His tells are omissions: he never asks the Hood's name; he never looks at
  the hood.
- Ayo is spoken of only as "the black one", "the thing under the hill", "what
  took the hunters". No one has her name before Act 3.
- The five colours are known lore ("five heads, five fires" in a nursery rhyme
  [Q23: provide one, or let the books invent it]) with no one alive who knows
  they were ever a family.
- After memory 30 the believer still lauds the wizard; the survivor still
  curses the Warmonger; the child still has their room. The irony is the
  player's alone. No neighbour "finds out" unless an ending says so.

7. THINGS THE GAME ALREADY HAS THAT NOW MEAN SOMETHING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- THE HOOD on the player sprite: never comes down, in any pose, any potion,
  any skin. The Dragon Powder minute (the player draws as a dragon) is a
  glimpse of the truth the player reads as a potion effect.
- THE SAFE AREA (only weak monsters near Home): the Hood's own leftover magic.
  The warden's "for some reason" is answered at memory 30.
- THE WIZARD TOWER'S RUNGS (power for memories): Tim rebuilding his general.
- THE DRAGON HUNTERS: the survivors' bravest, armed at the tower, lost in the
  caves. [Q24: do they appear as NPCs (recruits by the tower, a notice board of
  the missing) or only as story?]
- THE NIGHT'S GHOSTS and THE DEEP DEMONS: Ayo's wards (per Q17).
- THE RED DRAGON on cave level 9+: [Q25: is this a sibling, one of Ayo's wards,
  or a wild dragon of this world? Draft: a WILD dragon of the sixth planet —
  this world had dragons of its own, smaller and animal, and the lore's
  "vicious creatures" has a grain of them in it too.]
- SHRINE BOONS: the world's own small magic answering the Hood's.
- THE KERB RULE (the road is never a refuge for monsters, always for the
  player): the survivors' one true superstition, and a clue — the roads were
  spared because the general who burned the towns would not burn the roads
  they came by.
