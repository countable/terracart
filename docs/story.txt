DRAGON HOOD — THE STORY (basis document)
================================================================================
Status: v3 (Oct 2026), dragon speech and the tower attack added.
This file is the story bible the game is written against. Where it disagrees with copy in src/
(memory_story.js, play_tips.js, books, item text), THIS FILE wins and the copy
is to be brought in line. Numbers (which memory, which restoration, which cave
level) stay owned by the code; this file names the beats, not the values,
except where a beat IS a number the story needs (see ACT STRUCTURE).

Anything still marked [OPEN] is not yet canon; the short list is at the end.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. THE PREMISE IN ONE BREATH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You are the Hood: a quiet survivor who never takes the hood down, mending a
broken world one roof at a time. You are a dragon in a bipedal body, and you
do not know it. Nobody does. Fifty years ago your sister's spell took your
dragon body and your memories, caught in the heat of a battle between your
father, a five-headed hydra who conquers worlds through his children, and the
two children who would not burn this one. That battle broke most of the
planet. Your father walks it now as a kindly old wizard called Tim, raising
dragon hunters among the survivors, because he needs a living dragon of each
of his five colours to get his power back, and the two he needs most are his
own children. Your sister hides dragons deep under the earth so he never can,
and climbs to the surface to kill his hunters, which is why every survivor you
meet will tell you dragons are vicious things that ended the world.

The game is the mending. The story is what you remember while you mend.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE WORLD: THE SIXTH PLANET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The sixth planet: the sixth world Tiamat set out to take, the first he did
  not. Survivors have no name for it beyond "the world"; a scholar's book may
  call it "the sixth" without knowing what it is sixth of. [OPEN: a name.]
- What the player walks on is the real map: a neighbourhood going back to
  meadow, its houses wrecks, its roads intact, its street lamps dark. The
  survivors call that night THE BREAKING ("the night the roofs fell"). Fifty
  years on, trees stand in the lanes; the survivors are old, or the children
  of the ones who were. The place the player starts in is MENDING LANE; the
  name survives on a bent street sign and in the mouths of the neighbours.
- CAVES run beneath the whole surface, level after level. The first levels
  are danger; the deep ones are legend. Ayo's hidden dragons sit at the
  deepest levels a player can reach, and the hunts for them are the game's
  very late endgame (see ACT 3).
- SHRINES, GROVES, THE OLD STONES, THE SHORE: the sacred places predate the
  Breaking and were spared by it. Their keepers (the zone keepers, npc.js)
  are the only people who never left. The boons the shrines lend are the
  world's own small leftover magic, not a dragon's.
- THE ROADS AND LAMPS: the one thing the Breaking did not touch. The
  survivors' superstition holds that a road is safe ground (the game's kerb
  rule is that superstition made true).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE FAMILY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TIAMAT — "TIM"
  A hydra: five heads, five colours (red, white, and three more), a conqueror
  of worlds. He takes a world by sending his children ahead as generals; he
  had two, a red and a white, and each is one of his heads made flesh. His
  POWER is the five colours bound to him; without a living dragon of each,
  the heads are only shapes. Fifty years ago that power was stripped (not his
  memories) when he clashed with his children over this world's fate. He
  REMEMBERS EVERYTHING. He is the only one who does.
  He lives now in human form as a wise old wizard who goes by Tim: warm,
  patient, generous with lessons. He KNOWS the Hood is his child from the
  first look. He says nothing. He needs the five colours brought to him ALIVE
  and bound; his own two children are the two he cannot catch himself, so he
  raises DRAGON HUNTERS from the survivors and, above all, raises the Hood,
  who will one day carry the white dragon to him and be the red.
  Tim never lies in his own voice. Everything he says is true and incomplete.
  He needs the Hood STRONGER, so he encourages every battle: the wild
  factions in the caves and on the surface are mostly incidental creeps, but
  some of them are secretly HIS, sent against the Hood as sparring partners
  and against the survivors as reasons to come to his tower.

AYO — THE SISTER
  A WHITE dragon, the strongest will of the children. It was her spell, fifty
  years ago, that stripped Tiamat of his power, and in the heat of that battle
  it caught her brother too: the Hood's body and memory went with it, BY
  ACCIDENT, the Hood between her and the father when the spell landed. She has
  carried that for fifty years. She hides dragons of the other colours deep
  beneath the earth so Tiamat can never assemble five, and she comes up to the
  surface, rarely, to kill his dragon hunters and to look for him. She does
  not hide what she is when she comes. That is where the lore comes from: a
  white dragon seen over a burning hunters' camp, every decade or so, is why
  survivors say dragons are vicious things that ended the world.
  Ayo is an ENEMY the player can fight and an ALLY the player can reach: she
  is the terror of the deep until the player learns what she is protecting,
  and she is the whole of two of the three endings.
  [OPEN: does Ayo know the Hood survived, and where? Draft: she knows the
  spell spared the Hood's life but not where the body went; she has looked
  for fifty years, under the earth, in the wrong direction.]

THE HOOD — THE PLAYER
  A RED dragon. Six hundred years old of a fifteen-hundred-year span; fifty
  years is a season, and the human body has not aged a day, which is the
  first crack an old neighbour can notice (an elder who swears you have not
  changed since the first years after the Breaking). Once Tiamat's general on
  five worlds; the survivors of the fifth called them the WARMONGER and the
  name followed them here. Lost the full dragon body and all memory in Ayo's
  spell. Kept the MAGIC: the player's recovered memories ARE that magic
  coming back (the memory ledger), the shrines answer it, and the fire
  breath, when it returns, was always theirs.
  Wears the hood always. It hides what a human body cannot: unnaturally
  bright red hair, sharp teeth, a forked tongue. The Hood keeps the hood up
  by a habit older than their memory and never explains it; survivors assume
  a burn, a vow, grief. Nobody asks twice. The Hood does not know what is
  under it. The game never says it in the player's hearing until memory 30.

THERE ARE NO OTHER SIBLINGS. The dragons Ayo hides are dragons of this world
and of the five before, the other three colours, gathered and kept below.
This world had dragons of its own: smaller, animal, and the lore's "vicious
creatures" has a grain of them in it too. The red dragon of the deep caves
(level 9 and below) is one of these wild ones, not family.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. FIFTY YEARS AGO: THE BREAKING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What happened, in order (the truth; the player learns it out of order, see
ACT STRUCTURE):
1. Tiamat brought the family to the sixth planet as he had to five before:
   children first, the Warmonger (the Hood) at their head, fire over the first
   towns. The survivors' "night the roofs fell" began as the Hood's fire.
2. The children would not finish it. [OPEN: why this world and not the five
   before. Draft: Ayo had walked it as a human first, as she always scouted,
   and came back changed; she brought the Hood to see what she had seen. The
   Hood turned last, and turned hardest.]
3. Tiamat came down himself to end the argument. The clash of the hydra and
   his children over the capital is what broke the planet: not a war of
   armies, one family in one sky, and the ground under them. The roofs that
   the Hood's fire had not taken, the battle took; the Hood's fire was IN
   PART the cause, in the conflict with Tiamat, and the player's memories will
   not let them sort their share from his.
4. Ayo cast the spell. Its aim was Tiamat's POWER. It took that, and, with the
   Hood between them in the heat of it, it took the Hood's body and memory
   too. An accident. The one thing Ayo has never said aloud.
5. The backlash emptied the sky. Tiamat fell as a man. The Hood fell as a
   hooded stranger with no name. Ayo went below. Most of the world's people
   were already gone.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. NOW: THE THREE WHO WALK THE SURFACE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE HOOD mends. Peacefully, roof by roof, field by field. They have no idea
why the old roads feel familiar, why the wrecks look like something they
should apologise for, or why the hood stays up.

TIM recruits. His tower is a wreck like the rest until the player restores it
(the fifteenth restoration). Once it stands, he is simply THERE, as if he had
always been, warm and interested, and the memories the player brings him buy
power (the tower's rungs). He teaches the player to hunt. Every lesson is true
and every lesson serves him: a stronger Hood brings him his colours faster.
His DRAGON HUNTERS are the survivors' bravest, armed at his tower, and they go
into the caves after "the dragons that did this" and mostly do not come back,
because of Ayo. He tells the survivors Ayo did it. He is right.

AYO hides and hunts. Her dragons are deep. Her visits to the surface are rare
and terrible and are the whole of the dragon lore. The night's ghosts and the
cave's demons are NOT hers: they are the world's own creeps, incidental, and
some of them are Tim's. She is met in person only at the end.

THE SURVIVORS believe: the Warmonger burned the world (true), dragons are
vicious (half true), the wise wizard is their hope (false), and the hooded
stranger is one of them (false). Every neighbour's line and every book page
is written from inside that belief. See WHO MAY SAY WHAT.

5. ACT STRUCTURE (mapped onto the existing mechanics)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The engine of the story is the MEMORY LEDGER (src/memory_story.js): the first
find of each discoverable thing returns a memory. Scenes land on every third
memory. The gates below are the ones the code holds today; keep them.

ACT 1 — THE MENDING (memories 0–8, before the tower)
  The Hood wakes at the trailer with nothing. The warden explains the safe
  area (nobody knows why only weak monsters live here; the truth: the Hood's
  own leftover magic, which Tim will later learn to read). Neighbours return
  as memories do. The story neighbours by the trailer arrive by memory count:
  the believer (lauds the wizard: "restore his tower and he will save us"),
  the survivor (tells of the night the roofs fell, names the Warmonger), the
  child (homeless until the next restoration).
  Memory 3: a bell in the smoke, a child held, the name Warmonger. Memory 6: a
  still figure watching from a window (Tim, who was there). Memory 9: the
  rumour of a wise man "who may know why the old roads feel familiar".
  The player learns: the world was burned; a dragon did it; a wizard might
  help. Nothing says what the player is.

ACT 2 — THE LESSONS (the first tower, memories 9–21)
  The fifteenth restoration is Tim's tower. It is sealed until nine memories.
  Inside, Tim is kind, asks for memories, gives power. His lessons (the
  existing beats: "In the right order", "What remains useful", "An old
  habit", "Their little lives", "What comes next") are the hydra teaching the
  general: remember the victory, not the doorway; move when told; the small
  lives are not yours to carry. He is REBUILDING THE WARMONGER for his own
  use. The player's recovered memories contradict him one by one (the Act 2
  memory panels): the raised hand was braced against a door, not welcoming;
  the "good habit" was the grip of a handler; the mark on the map was a town.
  THE HUNTERS: dragon hunters are NPCs. They gather at the tower once it
  stands, go down into the caves in Tim's name, and are missed; a notice of
  the missing, a widow among the neighbours, a hunter met in a cave who has
  seen "the white one". They are the survivors' bravest and Tim's spent coin.
  THE SPARRING: Tim sends the Hood against the wild factions, and some of what
  the Hood fights is his (a garrison that should not be there, a foe that
  turns up when he has just said it would). Never stated; a reader who
  reads the books in order can work it out.
  THE WHITE ONE AT THE TOWER: midway through the wider Act 2 arc, at the
  existing first-tower departure gate (21 memories), Ayo attacks Tim's tower
  in full white-dragon form. The player witnesses the attack. Her shadow
  crosses the lamps; she strikes the tower, and the whole tower vanishes.
  The road and lamps remain. This replaces the quiet abandoned-tower visit:
  there is no empty room to enter, only the place where the tower stood.
  Tim survives and is next found at the second tower. The exact magic of the
  disappearance remains unexplained here; the attack causes it, rather than
  an unrelated move happening off screen. His invitation, "come to me when
  you are ready," reaches the Hood afterward, outside the vanished tower.
  The witnesses call the attacker "the white one". To them, the attack
  proves that the dragon hunters need their wizard more than ever. Nobody
  names Ayo or reveals the family; the Hood does not yet speak dragon.

ACT 2b — THE SECOND TOWER (restoration 25+, memories 21–30)
  The Hood restores the second tower among the wrecks after the first has
  vanished. Its hunters speak of the white dragon's attack. Tim's warmth is
  thinner; the map is open on his table ("this land will seem very small"). At memory
  30 he tells the Hood what they are and what he needs, plainly: you are a
  dragon, and so am I; the fire above the ruined town was your own; the
  Warmonger was you. And the ask: CAPTURE THE WHITE DRAGON, alive, and bring
  her to me, and my power is restored. He does not say she is their sister.
  He does not say the Hood is the red. He has three colours waiting below and
  needs two.

ACT 3 — THE FIRE BELOW (after memory 30; the hunts are very late game)
  The player goes down for their fire (cave level 9, the existing DragonStory
  reward) and keeps going. Below are Ayo's hidden dragons, one colour at a
  time, each bound in her keeping; the HUNTS are the capture of each, alive
  (the net and the catch wheel are the tools; a dragon that dies is a colour
  lost to everyone). And at the bottom, the white dragon.
  THE LANGUAGE COMES BACK: during Act 3, after the memory-30 revelation, a
  dragon speaks in the presence of the Hood and a surviving dragon hunter.
  The Hood understands and answers before thinking. The hunter lowers their
  weapon: "That was no spell. You spoke to it." The Hood is surprised too;
  the words feel older than any lesson Tim gave them. This is recovered
  dragon speech, not a skill Tim has just taught or a potion effect.
  Later, a second survivor hears the Hood answer a dragon and stops in
  astonishment: "It answered you. How did you know what to say?" People can
  learn that the Hood speaks dragon without learning that the Hood IS one.
  The exchange lets a dragon be heard as a person before the Hood chooses
  whether to capture or free it. Speaking grants understanding, not obedience
  or an automatic capture. The hood stays raised throughout.
  The player learns, from the hidden dragons and from Ayo herself, what Tim
  left out: that the white is their sister, that the spell was an accident,
  that the five colours bound to the hydra are the end of this world and the
  next.
  THREE ENDINGS:
  (a) THE GENERAL: deliver the colours and the white dragon to Tim. The hydra
      rises; the Hood's dragon body is given back by the father who took the
      family to five worlds; the sixth planet is his; the mending was for
      nothing. Playable, and the only ending with the dragon body restored.
  (b) THE SISTER: stand with Ayo. Free what the hunts bound, and face Tim; a
      man with all his memories and none of his power. The Hood keeps the
      hood and the fire and never gets the body back. The family stays
      broken so the world can stay mended.
  (c) THE PACIFIST: neither. Refuse the hunts, release every dragon the player
      caught, and go home to the lane. Tim is left waiting in a tower nobody
      visits; Ayo is left below with her charges; the Hood mends. Nothing is
      resolved and nothing is burned. The title's own ending.
  [OPEN: what becomes of Tim in (b): draft, not killed; left among the people
  he meant to burn, seen once more mending a roof.]

6. WHO MAY SAY WHAT (the spoiler gates)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hard rules, enforced by the tests (memory_arc, story_neighbours, books):
- Before memory 30, nothing in the player's hearing says the Hood is a dragon,
  that the wizard is their father, or that the wizard is choosing what to give
  back. Foreshadowing is allowed and encouraged: the familiar roads, the figure
  in the window, the elder who swears you have not aged, the hood nobody asks
  about, the safe area nobody can explain, the hair a child once glimpsed and
  was told was a trick of the light.
- "Dragons are vicious and ended the world" is what EVERY survivor believes
  and may say at any time. It is not a spoiler; it is the lore. Books, keepers,
  scouts, the believer, the hunters: all of them.
- "The Warmonger" is the name survivors have for the one who burned the roofs.
  Anyone may use it. No one knows the Warmonger was a general's title and not
  a dragon's name; a scholar may wonder.
- Tim never lies in his own voice. Everything he says is true and incomplete.
  His tells are omissions: he never asks the Hood's name; he never looks at
  the hood; he never says "sister".
- Ayo is spoken of only as "the white one", "the thing under the hill", "what
  took the hunters". No one has her name before Act 3.
- The five colours are known lore ("five heads, five fires", a nursery rhyme
  [OPEN: write one, or let the books invent it]) with no one alive who knows
  two of them were ever a family.
- After memory 30 the believer still lauds the wizard; the survivor still
  curses the Warmonger; the child still has their room. The irony is the
  player's alone. Witnesses in Act 3 may discover and react to the Hood's
  dragon speech; that does not reveal the Hood's dragon identity or family.
  No neighbour learns those secrets unless an ending says so.

7. THINGS THE GAME ALREADY HAS THAT NOW MEAN SOMETHING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- THE HOOD on the player sprite: never comes down, in any pose, any potion,
  any skin. The Dragon Powder minute (the player draws as a dragon) is a
  glimpse of the truth the player reads as a potion effect.
- THE SAFE AREA (only weak monsters near Home): the Hood's own leftover magic.
  The warden's "for some reason" is answered at memory 30.
- THE WIZARD TOWER'S RUNGS (power for memories): Tim rebuilding his general.
- THE DRAGON HUNTERS: NPCs. The survivors' bravest, armed at the tower, lost
  in the caves; a notice board of the missing; a hunter's widow; a hunter
  deep down who has seen the white one.
- THE NIGHT'S GHOSTS, THE LAIRS, THE DEEP DEMONS: the world's own creeps,
  incidental; some of them Tim's, never announced.
- THE RED DRAGON on cave level 9+: a wild dragon of this world, not family.
- THE NET AND THE CATCH WHEEL: the hunts' tools. Alive, or lost.
- SHRINE BOONS: the world's own small magic answering the Hood's.
- THE KERB RULE (the road is never a refuge for monsters, always for the
  player): the survivors' one true superstition, and a clue: the roads were
  spared because the general who burned the towns would not burn the roads
  they came by.

8. NAMES AND SPELLINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Game title: DRAGON HOOD. The neighbourhood is MENDING LANE. (Storage keys
  stay terracart.*.)
- TIAMAT, "Tim". AYO. The Hood (the player is never named; "the Hood" in
  prose; survivors say "the hooded one", "stranger", "neighbour").
- The Warmonger: the title, never a name.
- The Breaking: the survivors' word for fifty years ago.
- Colours: the Hood red, Ayo white; the hidden dragons the other three
  [OPEN: which three; draft blue, green, black].
